# an-style — script to cut-out animation in the style of X

A style here is a file: `styles/<name>.yaml` beside this skill, one per style. Each has three parts, and the difference between them is the whole point:

- **`live`** — settings that map onto shipped `an` features. Apply every one. `tests/test_style_specs.py` checks each key against the code (a real `Meta`, a `StylePack` whose roles are reachable, camera moves and easings that exist, a valid `EnvironmentDescriptor`), so if it is in `live`, `an` can do it.
- **`targets`** — `[low, high]` ranges for statistics measured on a render by `an.verify.style` (the style lint). Measured on one short clip per style, so they are starting points, not definitions.
- **`guidance`** — what the style needs beyond the `live` settings: how to build it from shipped pieces where that takes hand work (a date card is an overlay text prop over a black fill plane; a map is fill planes plus path and text props — the `an` skill's *A project from nothing* builds both), and what `an` does NOT do yet (moves with no `an.motion` preset, morph/match-move transitions, and the sound ASSETS themselves: `an` ships no music or SFX library). Build what is buildable; **tell the user which of the rest were omitted**. Never imply the render has them. `tests/test_style_specs.py` fails if a guidance line calls a shipped feature (text, paths, planes, sounds, transitions) missing.

Styles shipped: `south_park`, `oversimplified`, `kurzgesagt`, `gilliam`, `reiniger`, `norstein` — the files are in `styles/` in THIS skill's directory (below, `<skill>/styles/<name>.yaml`). The measurements and sources behind them are in the `an` repo's `misc/docs/cutout_styles_research.md` (background; not needed to apply a spec). For the scene format, the asset recipe (text, paths, planes, StylePacks, sounds), units and a complete starting `scene.md`, the `an` skill is the reference.

## The procedure

1. **Pick the spec** and **state its `cost_class` to the user before starting**, with its `cost_note`. `low` (South Park) and `low_to_medium` (OverSimplified) are shape vocabularies an agent can generate with `an character new --offline`. `high` and `very_high` (Reiniger, Kurzgesagt, Norstein) mean the art is the cost: `an` can stage and time them, but offline characters are stand-ins, and the user should know that before a render, not after. Also list the `guidance` items that will be missing.
2. **Set the meta** from `live.meta` into the ` ```yaml meta ` block: `fps`, `step_hz` when the spec has one (at 24 fps, `12` is on twos; the camera, blinks, swap channels including mouths, and descriptor `play` clips are never stepped), `default_easing` (the style's house curve — `linear` for South Park, `step` for Gilliam; every tween that names no easing takes it), and `style_pack` when the spec has one.
3. **Save the StylePack** from `live.style_pack` as `assets/styles/<name>.json`: `mall["styles"][name] = StylePack(**live["style_pack"]).model_dump(mode="json")` with `mall = an.stores.build_project_mall(<dir>)`. Know its reach: roles recolour environment presets, procedural rigs and **every `an character new --offline` character** (its descriptor tags its colours by role). Hand-drawn art is untagged and keeps its colours, and a DiceBear character's skin and hair stay its own; the compiler says so in one line. Colours the spec lists under `guidance` (accents, card backgrounds) are not StylePack roles; the reachable roles are `skin`, `clothing`, `hair`, `leg`, `pupil`, `accessory`, `sky`, `ground`, and `stroke` (a path prop that sets no `color` of its own). The pack's `surface` (outline, paper-gap shadow) and `grain` are a different mechanism: compiled copies and textures that reach every character and prop, tagged or not (an#163). When the spec has `live.glow_template` (Kurzgesagt), add it to the pack as `entity_surfaces: {<id>: <that template>}` for each entity that should glow, not to every entity. A treated character darkens when faded (`alpha` below 1): switch its treatment off with `entity_surfaces: {<id>: {outline: false, shadow: false}}` for a long fade.
4. **Set the environment** from `live.environment`: a `preset` goes in the entity's `ref`; a `descriptor` is saved as `assets/environments/<name>/meta.json` — `mall["environments"][name] = EnvironmentDescriptor(**descriptor).model_dump(mode="json")` — and referenced by that name. Cards, maps and other sets the structure calls for are more plane environments of your own, built the same way. Plane `depth` is a parallax ratio (1.0 = character plane); a zoom is uniform across planes, so pair far plates with pans.
5. **Cut the script into shots** to `live.shots` (`mean_s`, `range_s`) and to the `cuts_per_min` target: shots = script duration × cuts_per_min / 60, roughly. Every shot boundary is `live.transitions.default` (a hard cut unless the spec says otherwise; step 8). Give each shot `camera: {move: <live.camera.default>}` and use only moves in `live.camera.allowed`. Follow the coverage and structure hints in `guidance` (two-shot then single close for South Park; narrated map, dramatization, date card for OverSimplified). **Cut on the style's rhythm, not on every beat**: a pause, a look or a reaction inside an exchange is a `(pause 1.5)` on the dialogue line plus an action with a `start` in the same shot (the `an` skill's *Dialogue timing*), not a new shot — one shot per beat is how an end-user run of a four-beat exchange measured 17–19 cuts/min against South Park's 10–14.
6. **Make the characters** per `live.characters`: `generate: offline` is `an character new <name> --offline --mouth-variants <live.characters.mouth_variants, comma-joined>` run from the project directory (it writes to `./assets/characters`; or pass `--out-dir <dir>/assets/characters`), plus the spec's factory knobs — `build` (`--build`), `head_scale` (`--head-scale`), one `hat` per character from the listed choices (`--hat`), `sash` (`--sash`, on the figures that wear one), and `palette: per_character` meaning each character gets its own costume (`--palette clothing=#..,accessory=#..,leg=#..`) — the costume and the hat are what tell figures apart. Never regex-edit generated `parts/*.svg`. A dialogue `[angry]` wants a `viseme@angry` set: add `angry` to the variants, or `an character mouths <name> --variants angry` later; `generate: promote` means hand-cut SVG art promoted with `an.characters.promote` (see `examples/promote_demo/`). `tint` is a `set` of the `tint` property on each character root at `at: 0` (black makes a silhouette). Frame them with `stage: {at: [x, y], scale: s}` on the entity; small characters barely move the frame statistics, so close framing matters for the cadence targets too.
7. **Author the motion** with tween lengths inside `live.tween_duration_s`; leave `easing` off a tween to take the scene's `default_easing`, and name one from `live.easing` only where the move wants a different curve (an overshoot pop-in). Cadence comes from the pattern of motion and holds, not from `step_hz` alone: South Park is short snaps on twos between holds while a character talks; OverSimplified is bursts on ones then long holds; Kurzgesagt is never still. Dialogue `[emotion]` tags drive brows, lids and the mouth form. `live.motion_presets` names the style's moves in `an.motion` (`pop_in`, `hop`, `shake`, `nod`, `point`, `slide_in`, `slide_out`, `squash_stretch`, `waddle`): write each as a `play` of its name in the ` ```yaml actions ` block — `{kind: play, target: stan, animation: waddle, args: {steps: 4, travel: 120}, start: 0.5}` — which builds it at the character's own rest pose (the `an` skill's *Motion presets* has the rules); presets keep their own easings and are stepped by `step_hz` like any tween. `guidance.motion_vocabulary_unmapped` lists the moves with no preset. **`live.motion_presets` is advisory** — the style's signature moves, not a whitelist: any preset in `an.motion.PRESETS` is fine in any style (a `hop` in OverSimplified is not a violation), and nothing checks the list against a scene. Units: `rotation` is radians, `hop` `height` is scene pixels, and an offline character is about 265 px tall at `stage.scale: 1` — the `an` skill's *Units and staging* has the framing arithmetic that keeps a hopping head in frame.
8. **Transitions and sound** (an#163): put `live.transitions.default` on every shot after the first as `transition:` in its ` ```yaml shot ` (omit it when the default is `cut`; the other named entries, like Kurzgesagt's `flash`, are for the shots that call for them). A dissolve shortens the film by its duration, so re-check `live.shots` against the overlap. When the spec has `live.sound.bed`, add the user's music to the `sounds` store (`an.sounds.add_sound(mall["sounds"], "bed", wav, source=...)` with its real licence) and put `{sound: bed, **live.sound.bed}` in the meta's `sounds`. SFX go on the shot as `sounds: [{sound: <key>, at: <shot-local s>}]`. **Never ship a track whose licence you have not recorded**; with no asset, render without the bed and tell the user (for a draft, `an.sounds.synth_bed` is a clearly-fake stand-in).
8b. **Voice** (an#163): when the spec has `live.voice.effects` (South Park: `pitch_semitones: 4`), put it in the `effects` key of each character's voice document (`mall["voices"]["<voice_ref>"] = {..., "effects": {...}}`) and give each dialogue line that `voice_ref`. It is applied to the synthesized audio before lip-sync, keeps the duration, and re-renders only the effect if you change it. It needs ffmpeg; an effect on a voice that does not exist in the store does nothing, and `an validate` flags an unknown or out-of-range effect. Tell the user the shifter is stock ffmpeg (a crude pitch-and-tempo chain).
9. **Render**: `an render <dir> --strict-assets`.
10. **Measure**: `python -m an.verify.style <dir>/output/main.mp4 <skill>/styles/<name>.yaml` prints every metric beside its target, one warning per miss with the knob that moves it (never one the spec forbids), and a **per-shot table** (held-frame share and cadence per shot — the way to find the one static shot holding a clip's share up). A video at `<dir>/output/*.mp4` finds its own project and takes the cuts from the authored shot list, exactly (dissolves included); any other video needs `--project <dir>`, or the cuts are detected from pixels, which misses a cut between two shots on the same backdrop — it warns when it falls back. `--json` for machine output. In Python: `an.verify.style.style_lint(mp4, spec, scene=<dir>)` (`.per_shot` holds the table), or `StyleLintVerifier(spec)` as a `Verifier`.
11. **Adjust and re-render** on the misses, then report the final numbers to the user next to the targets, and say which misses remain and why.

## Reading the lint

- `identical_frame_share` too high: more motion while characters talk (gesture beats of 0.25–0.3 s), closer framing, fewer dead holds. Too low: shorter tweens and real holds between moves — plus `step_hz` only if the spec sets one (the lint never suggests a `step_hz` change the spec forbids).
- `one_frame_interval_share` too low for OverSimplified or Gilliam: check `step_hz` is unset (their specs leave it unset). Too high for South Park: check `step_hz: 12` is set.
- **The share moves in big steps; tune in small ones.** In the e2e OverSimplified run, adding one small hop to every gesture burst took `identical_frame_share` from 0.677 to 0.529 (target 0.55–0.65), and changing the burst period from 0.8 s to 0.72 s then moved it back only to 0.545. Change one knob at a time by about half what you think, and read the per-shot table to see which shot moved. A static shot (a date card, a held map) is about 0.9 identical on its own and weighs by its length: a 2.2 s card in a 19 s clip added about 0.05 to the whole-clip share.
- `cuts_per_min` and `mean_shot_s` are coarse on a short clip: under 30 s, one cut moves `cuts_per_min` by 60 / duration, and the lint says so. An 8 s clip cannot hit South Park's 10–14 cuts/min and 3–5.5 s mean shot at once; report that rather than contort the edit.
- Palette targets (`mean_saturation`, `dark_pixel_share`, `top16_colour_coverage`) move with the backdrop (StylePack `sky`/`ground`, plane fills) and the art, not with timing.
- The estimator sees change as mean grey difference over a 320×180 frame. A mouth swap on a small character is below its threshold: that is how the targets were measured too, so it is consistent, but it means lip-sync alone cannot lower the held-frame share.

## Worked example

The `an` repo's `misc/demos/build_demos.py` `south-park-style` demo applies `styles/south_park.yaml` to a four-line script (two 4 s shots: a two-shot, then a single close), renders it, and lints it. It predates `an.motion` and `default_easing`, so its moves are hand-written tweens with their easings spelled out. Its first pass with only the `live` settings and default framing measured `identical_frame_share` 0.96 against a 0.55–0.70 target; closer framing plus arm-and-head gesture beats while each character talks brought it to about 0.64, inside the range. `cuts_per_min` stays at 7.5 (one cut in 8 s), a structural miss of a short clip.

## What never to do

- Never put a `guidance` item in the scene as if it worked (a `voice_fx` key, an `outline` role, a `wipe` transition). An outline is `surface.outline` in the pack, never a colour role. Name every one you could not express to the user as omitted.
- Never claim a render "is in the style of X" without the lint numbers beside the targets.
- Never commit study footage of a real show or film anywhere (copyright). The targets came from clips kept outside every repository.
- Never tune a spec's `targets` to make a render pass. Targets change only with a new measurement of the real style, recorded in the research doc.
