an.ir.frames#

Frames of reference: a tree of named poses over film time (ADR 0009 amendment, an#501).

A frame is a rigid pose (a position and an orientation) as a function of film time, relative to a parent frame; world is the root. Every element of the scene’s world (a member, a viewpoint, a stream, a plane) is in exactly one frame, with a local pose in that frame’s units. Its world pose is the composition of the poses up the chain, and what a viewpoint sees is the pinhole projection of world poses into the viewpoint’s own frame (the projection itself is an.ir.world.Projection). The multiplane camera, the moving panorama, the process shot and the camera rig are all the same tree with a different shape: what stands still in one frame is scenery in another, and the eye is attached to whichever frame the shot is in.

Orientation uses the viewpoint’s convention: look: [yaw, pitch, roll] degrees, the frame’s local axes being (right, down, forward) — so a frame with look zero is the world’s axes (x right, y DOWN, z away from the viewer).

A far frame follows its parent’s rotation and none of its translation: what is at infinity turns with the camera and never moves with it.

This module is pure geometry on tuples; it imports the schema’s Frame and the easing function, nothing else. It is an extraction candidate: previz (an#257) is the TypeScript twin of the same vocabulary.

>>> from an.ir.schema import Frame, FrameMoving
>>> tree = FrameTree({
...     "car": Frame(moving=FrameMoving(speed=20.0)),
...     "seat": Frame(parent="car", at=(0.45, 0.65, 1.6)),
...     "horizon": Frame(parent="car", coupling="far"),
... })
>>> tree.world_pose("seat", 2.0).at          # 2 s on: the seat is 40 m down the road
(0.45, 0.65, 41.6)
>>> tree.world_pose("horizon", 2.0).at       # at infinity: never translates
(0.0, 0.0, 0.0)

class an.ir.frames.FrameTree(frames, *, tracks=None)[source]#

Bases: object

The frames of a scene and the world pose of anything in one of them.

tracks are the shot’s set/tween actions on frame channels ({frame: {channel: Track}}, shot time): a track REPLACES the channel’s declared value for that shot, the way a pose value replaces a property. moving adds direction × speed × film time to the position on top. Poses are evaluated at a shot time t with film_start the shot’s start in the film, so a moving frame keeps its phase across a cut.

>>> from an.ir.schema import Frame, FrameMoving
>>> tree = FrameTree({"car": Frame(moving=FrameMoving(speed=10.0))})
>>> tree.world_pose("car", 1.0, film_start=4.0).at
(0.0, 0.0, 50.0)
>>> tree.relative_pose("world", "car", 1.0).at        # the world seen from the car: it comes at us
(0.0, 0.0, -10.0)
>>> FrameTree({"a": Frame(parent="b"), "b": Frame(parent="a")})

acceleration(name, t, *, film_start=0.0, dt=0.01, within=None)[source]#

The world acceleration of name’s origin at shot time t (second difference of step dt, units/s²): zero for a frame at rest or at a constant speed.

within=(lo, hi) keeps the three samples inside that window (a shot): near an end the stencil slides inward (a one-sided difference), and a window narrower than 2·dt shrinks the step to half of it, so a motion that starts at the window’s edge is not read as an impulse.

A step of the frame (jump_times()) is a cut, not an acceleration: a stencil that would span one slides to the nearest centre that stays whole, and where none does the answer is zero.

>>> from an.ir.schema import Frame, FrameMoving
>>> tree = FrameTree({"car": Frame(moving=FrameMoving(speed=20.0))})
>>> [round(v, 3) + 0.0 for v in tree.acceleration("car", 1.0)]
[0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
>>> track = Track([TrackEvent(0.0, 1.0, to=10.0, from_=0.0, easing="ease_out")])
>>> braking = FrameTree({"car": Frame()}, tracks={"car": {"at_z": track}})
>>> [round(v, 1) + 0.0 for v in braking.acceleration("car", 0.5)]   # 20 m/s to 0 in a second
[0.0, 0.0, -20.0]
>>> [round(v, 1) + 0.0 for v in braking.acceleration("car", 0.0, dt=0.04, within=(0.0, 1.0))]
[0.0, 0.0, -20.0]
