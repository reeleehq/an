an.ir.holds#

Holding things: grasp, place, release, handover and swap (an#502, catalogue A4).

A hold says what carries an item over a span of a shot: a hand (any point of a body), a socket of another body (a figurine’s hand), the ground, a point of the stage, or nothing (it is in flight). These five action kinds change holds, and each one moves the holder the way a hand would, so the change reads as handled rather than teleported:

  • grasp: the holder’s point (by) reaches the item’s grip over reach seconds, takes it at the instant it arrives (the item keeps the pose it has at that instant: no slide, no jump), and retracts to where the shot would have it over retract seconds, carrying it. The item is taken from whatever held it: the ground, a socket, another hand (a hand-over between two hands is the receiver’s grasp).

  • place: the hand that holds the item brings it onto a target over reach seconds (its contact point onto the ground at floor height, its grip into a socket, aligned with the socket’s frame), lets it go there, and retracts empty.

  • release: the hand lets go at at; the item falls under gravity with the velocity it had and lands on the ground at its rest angle, or is tossed onto: ground@x on an arc of height arc.

  • handover: place into another holder’s point (to), or, with meet below 1, both hands reach toward each other and the receiver takes it where they meet.

  • swap: a hand takes the item a holder has (take) and the hand holding another item gives it in its place (give), a beat later.

A reach that cannot arrive is never cheated: the body it hangs from may bend (lean, degrees; the chain’s root turns about its own pivot, the way a figure bends at the hip), and beyond that an validate says how far the hand stays short and where to stand instead.

None of these makes a clip: the solve compile pass reads them (an.stage.hold) and emits the holders’ and the items’ keys.

>>> from an.ir.compose import flatten, sequence, delay
>>> [(f.start, f.end, f.action.kind) for f in flatten(sequence(delay(1.0), grasp("paul/arm_r", "cup")))]
[(1.0, 1.75, 'grasp')]
>>> [(f.start, f.action.kind) for f in flatten(sequence(delay(2.0), release("cup", at=0.5)))]
[(2.5, 'release')]
>>> [(round(f.start, 2), f.action.kind) for f in flatten(swap("nap/hand", take="musket", give="sword", by="stu@hand_r"))]
[(0.0, 'grasp'), (0.25, 'place')]

These kinds are the core’s (ADR 0001’s test: a robot arm putting a box on a shelf needs them without saying character), registered when an.ir loads.

Bases: ExtensionAction

by (a body’s point: a hand) reaches item’s grip and takes it.

item is an entity id, with @<point> the grip (a bone of its rig, or u,v a fraction of its drawing); unset: its grip bone, else its stage point. by with no @ is the far end of the body (an arm’s hand). The item is held from start + reach on, and over the retract it turns to how the hand holds things (align): drawn as it is, upright along the hand’s part, the way a socket holds it; align: false keeps the angle it was taken at.

Bases: ExtensionAction

The hand holding item puts it onto a target and lets it go.

onto is ground (straight down, at floor height), ground@x (at stage x), a body’s point (a socket: the item’s grip goes there, its frame aligned with the socket’s) or world@x,y (hung on a point). item@<point> names the item’s point that touches the target (default: its grip on a socket, its lowest point on the ground). rotation: the item’s rotation where it rests (radians, the stage’s; default: the angle it last rested at on the ground, else the nearest quarter turn; in a socket, the socket’s own). In a socket it is the angle the item comes to rest at (a musket aimed at someone, a sabre raised), held relative to the socket from then on, so it turns with it.
