an.kinematics#

A deterministic 2D constraint solver: bodies joined at points, driven at a few of them, settled by gravity (an#499, ADR 0008).

Four nouns and one verb. A Body is a rigid rectangle posed by (x, y, rotation, scale); a Point on it is a fraction of its extent. A joint relates two bodies (or a body and WORLD): Pin, Hinge (a pin with an angle range), Weld, Distance (equal a rod, at_most a string, at_least a spacer), Slide (a groove) and Contact (one-sided: a foot on the ground, a lamp on a table). A driver is a target on a body’s channels (a nail: met exactly where the joints allow), a Force (a push, or a soft pull toward a point), or gravity. solve() does one frame, solve_frames() a run of them with the caller’s targets and joined/unjoined joints per frame, and both return a typed result that names what could not be held instead of raising.

>>> torso = Body("torso", width=40.0, height=80.0)
>>> upper = Body("upper", x=20.0, y=-30.0, width=50.0, height=10.0, origin=(0.0, 0.5))
>>> fore = Body("fore", x=70.0, y=-30.0, width=40.0, height=8.0, origin=(0.0, 0.5))
>>> handle = Body("handle", x=80.0, y=10.0, width=4.0, height=4.0, fixed=True)
>>> arm = [Pin(Point("upper", 0.0, 0.5), Point("torso", 1.0, 0.125)),
...        Hinge(Point("fore", 0.0, 0.5), Point("upper", 1.0, 0.5), limits=(-2.6, 0.0)),
...        Pin(Point("fore", 1.0, 0.5), Point("handle", 0.5, 0.5))]
>>> solve([torso, upper, fore, handle], arm, targets={"torso": {"x": 0.0, "y": 0.0}}).ok
True

Nothing here imports the scene IR or the stage, and nothing names an animation: bodies are numbers, joints are rows (# extraction candidate: this becomes its own package when a second consumer exists). The compile pass that reads joints from a scene is P2 (an#512).

The method, and why (measured in misc/docs/research/puppeteering_2026-10.md §4, which compared analytic two-bone, CCD, FABRIK, PBD and XPBD on the same tasks, and re-measured on this module by misc/docs/research/puppeteering_2026-10/measure_kinematics.py, results in kinematics_measurements.json beside it; CPython 3.12, x86-64, one core). Extended position-based dynamics on 2D rigid bodies (XPBD, Macklin et al. 2016; rigid bodies and joints, Müller et al. 2020): Gauss–Seidel over the joints in a fixed order, each a projection with the generalized inverse mass 1/m + (r × n)² / I about the body’s centre (or about its pose point while that is held), hinge limits as angular projections, a fixed count of substeps and iterations. It is the one family of the five that does chains, strings, rods, contacts, gravity and unjoining in one loop, and its compliance is a stiffness that does not depend on the iteration count. Its weakness, slow propagation down long chains, is covered for the common case by the analytic two-link solve used as the initial guess for every chain between two held ends (an arm with a hand is a two-link chain whose second link is the forearm and the hand held at their current angles), which also makes the elbow’s bend side an explicit choice (the side its range allows, else the side it already has).

  • Convergence per iteration (the arm above, pose mode, worst residual): from the closed-form start, 2e-14 px after one pass; from a cold start, 5.8 / 3.3 / 0.80 / 0.10 / 0.0014 px after 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 / 16 passes, 5e-14 after 64. A frame that leaves a joint failing is retried with four times the passes, so the default eight reach the sixteen-pass figure when needed.

  • Singularities: a target at exactly full reach (a straight arm) is met with zero residual and a straight elbow; 1 px or 5 px beyond reach the grip alone is refused (dropped, its gap in unsatisfied), the shoulder and elbow hold exactly, and the arm points straight at the target; a target on the shoulder itself (inside the nearest reach, where the direction is undefined) is refused the same way, the arm left where it was rather than guessed. (The torso is nailed in rotation here; free to turn, it turns toward the target first.) A chain between two rigidly held ends whose ends are outside its reach is refused before any pass.

  • A straight chain pushed along its length (a rope bridge of 3 to 20 planks pinned at 0.8 of its length, laid straight): no correction has a sideways part, so nothing moves; the retry’s start, nudged by 1e-3 rad, buckles it, and every joint holds (tests).

  • Stability at frame-exact steps (a 12-link chain dropped horizontal, 10 s at 24 fps, under gravity): at 4 substeps (the default) no frame fails a joint and the tip hangs within 0.03° of vertical; at 1 or 2 substeps the whip under-converges (worst 3.2 / 0.9 px with the retry, 17 / 3.4 px without), reported per frame and never given up (a tree cannot conflict). Nothing diverges at any setting.

  • Compliance: a soft rod (compliance=1e-4) under a 100-unit weight stretches 11.85 units after one second at 1, 2, 8 and 32 iterations alike, to the digit; it settles to the static 9.8.

  • Cost per frame, dynamic, defaults: 14 ms for five ten-part marionettes (50 bodies, 65 joints, 25 strings), no frame failing; 0.9 ms for the two-link reach in pose mode. A 50-link rope or chain costs 34-48 ms (the retry runs on its failing frames) and under-converges on 40-47 of 48 frames (Gauss–Seidel’s n² propagation): reported, never cut. Long ropes want more substeps, or the FABRIK seam the research names.

  • Determinism: Python floats and math inside the loop, numpy at the boundary; bitwise-equal poses across runs and across processes on one platform (tests/test_kinematics.py). Not across platforms: a dynamic run amplifies a last-ulp libm difference (1e-13 on one frame’s input is 0.014 units 96 frames later), so a cache key is built on the inputs (inputs_digest(), with SOLVER_VERSION), never the output.

Not in this version: friction (a contact is frictionless: a lamp on a table sliding sideways stays where it is until the edge passes), blending a snap (a rejoined joint closes its gap in one frame, reported in FramesResult.snaps), and the stochastic driver seed is reserved for.

an.kinematics.solve(bodies, joints, *, targets=None, forces=(), gravity=None, dt=None, iterations=8, substeps=4, damping=0.02, tolerance=0.25, angle_tolerance=0.001, seed_chains=True, retry=4, drop_conflicts=True, seed=0)[source]#

One frame: the poses of all bodies that satisfy the joined joints with the targets held, forces and gravity acting.

dt=None is pose mode: no inertia, no gravity, no pushes (a gravity or a Force.vector is refused there: they need time; a Force.toward pull works in both modes). With a dt the bodies step through substeps of dt / substeps, carrying their velocities in and out, and a target is reached over those substeps. targets hold channels exactly (a body held in x and y is immovable to the joints for the frame, one held in rotation unturnable); a target beyond a chain’s reach shows up in unsatisfied rather than stretching anything. Hinge and weld rests left None are the relative angles the bodies have on input. A frame that leaves a joint failing is solved again with retry times the iterations (1: never); drop_conflicts then gives up the joints in conflict, last declared first (module docstring); False keeps them all and reports what fails. seed is reserved for stochastic drivers and does nothing in this version: the solve has no randomness.

A shoulder driven to a point, an upper arm pinned to it, a forearm hinged to that, the hand pinned to a handle (the two-link case): the hand lands on the handle and the elbow bends the way its range allows.

>>> torso = Body("torso", width=40.0, height=80.0)
>>> upper = Body("upper", x=20.0, y=-30.0, width=50.0, height=10.0, origin=(0.0, 0.5))

Out of reach, the grip is reported, and the arm stays on its shoulder:

>>> far = handle.posed(x=300.0)
>>> r = solve([torso, upper, fore, far], joints, targets={"torso": {"x": 0.0, "y": 0.0}})
>>> [j for j, _ in r.unsatisfied], r.dropped, [round(v, 6) for v in r.residuals[:2]]
([2], (2,), [0.0, 0.0])

an.kinematics.solve_frames(bodies, joints, *, frames, dt, targets_at=None, joints_at=None, forces=(), gravity=None, **options)[source]#

frames solves in a row, each starting from the last: the caller’s time dimension. targets_at(k) (or targets_at[k]) gives frame k’s targets; joints_at(k) its joints (the same list with joined flags flipped is how a joint is unjoined and rejoined). Hinge and weld rests are fixed from the starting poses. options go to solve().

A part unjoined falls, and is caught where it is when rejoined:

>>> peg = Body("peg", width=2.0, height=2.0, fixed=True)
>>> part = Body("part", y=10.0, width=4.0, height=20.0, origin=(0.5, 0.0))
>>> joined = [Pin(Point("part", 0.5, 0.0), Point("peg", 0.5, 0.5))]
>>> loose = [Pin(Point("part", 0.5, 0.0), Point("peg", 0.5, 0.5), joined=False)]
>>> r = solve_frames([peg, part], joined, frames=12, dt=1 / 24, gravity=(0.0, 980.0),
...                  joints_at=lambda k: loose if 2 <= k < 8 else joined)
>>> bool(r.poses[1, 1, 1] < r.poses[7, 1, 1]), r.snaps[0][:2], r.snaps[0][2] > 2.0
(True, (8, 0), True)
>>> r.unsatisfied[-1]

an.kinematics.two_bone(root, target, lengths, *, bend=1)[source]#

Absolute angles (theta1, theta2) of two links of lengths (l1, l2) hinged at root so that the tip meets target; None when the target is out of reach().

bend is +1 for a middle joint that deviates counter-clockwise from the root-to-target line (which, on a y-down screen, reads as clockwise), -1 for the other side. Each side is a different pose of the same chain; which one is right is the author’s (or a hinge range’s) choice.

>>> t1, t2 = two_bone((0.0, 0.0), (1.0, 1.0), (1.0, 1.0), bend=1)
>>> round(math.degrees(t1)), round(math.degrees(t2))
(90, 0)
>>> t1, t2 = two_bone((0.0, 0.0), (1.0, 1.0), (1.0, 1.0), bend=-1)
>>> round(math.degrees(t1)), round(math.degrees(t2))
(0, 90)
