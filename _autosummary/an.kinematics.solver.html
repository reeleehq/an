an.kinematics.solver#

The solve: bodies joined at points, driven at a few, settled by gravity.

One frame at a time (solve()), or a run of frames with the caller’s targets and joined/unjoined joints per frame (solve_frames()). The method and the measurements behind it are in the package docstring (an.kinematics); in one line: extended position-based dynamics on 2D rigid bodies [Müller et al. 2020], Gauss–Seidel over the joints, a fixed number of substeps and iterations, hinge limits as angular projections, the two-link closed form as the initial guess.

Two modes. With dt=None (pose) the bodies are projected onto the joints from where they stand, with no inertia and no gravity: the answer is a function of the targets alone (plus the starting poses, which choose between symmetric solutions). With a dt (dynamic) the bodies carry velocity from frame to frame, gravity and forces act, a target is reached over the frame’s substeps (the driven body moves, it does not teleport), and the result depends on every frame before: baked from the first frame at a fixed substep, it is still a pure function of the inputs.

Conflicts. When the joints cannot all hold (a grip beyond the arm’s reach, an elbow asked past its range), Gauss–Seidel would spread the error over every joint in the loop and tear the arm off its shoulder. The rows are projected last-declared first, so the first-declared joint is the last correction of every pass. A frame that leaves a rigid joint failing is solved again with DFLT_CONFLICT_RETRY times the iterations from a start nudged by DFLT_NUDGE (a straight chain pushed along its length has no sideways correction to buckle with otherwise). If that at least halves the failure (DFLT_CONFLICT_PROGRESS) it was slow convergence, and what is left is only reported. If not, it is a conflict, settled per LOOP: the joints that share a cycle through the held bodies with a failing one are tried last-declared first, and the first whose loss lets every other joint hold is given up for the frame (a joint on no cycle, such as any joint of a rope hung from one hook, is never a candidate: a tree can always be satisfied). The joints given up are in SolveResult.dropped, and their gaps in unsatisfied. So declare the structure (the skeleton) before what it reaches for (a grip): an unreachable grip is refused, and the arm reaches toward it from its shoulder.

Determinism. Fixed order, fixed counts, no randomness, Python floats and math only inside the loop (Gauss–Seidel is sequential, and scalar numpy is both slower and free to take a different SIMD path per CPU), numpy only at the boundary: on one platform the same inputs give the same bytes, run after run and process after process. Across platforms they need not: a libm may differ in the last ulp, and a dynamic run amplifies that (measured: 1e-13 on one frame’s input is 0.014 units 96 frames later). So a cache key built on a solve is built on its INPUTS (inputs_digest(), which holds SOLVER_VERSION), never on its output; quantize() and digest() are for storing and comparing poses on one platform.

>>> arm = Body("arm", x=0.0, y=0.0, width=60.0, height=8.0, origin=(0.0, 0.5))
>>> r = solve([arm], [Pin(Point("arm", 0.0, 0.5), Point(WORLD, 0.0, 0.0))],
...           targets={"arm": {"rotation": 0.5}})
>>> round(r.pose("arm").rotation, 3), r.unsatisfied
(0.5, ())

DFLT_ITERATIONS

"smaller time steps are more effective than more iterations" (Catto 2024), and four keeps a 24 fps frame under a quarter of its period of free fall at 980 px/s².

DFLT_DAMPING

the one style knob a puppet has for how long it swings.

DFLT_TOLERANCE

A joined joint whose points are further apart than this after the solve is reported as unsatisfied (scene units; a quarter pixel is below what a render shows).

DFLT_ANGLE_TOLERANCE

A hinge or weld whose angle is outside its range by more than this is reported (radians; 1e-3 rad is 0.06 degrees).

DFLT_CONFLICT_RETRY

A solve that leaves a joint failing is run again with this many times the iterations before anything is given up: what more passes fix was slow convergence (a hard swing), what they do not is a conflict.

DFLT_CONFLICT_PROGRESS

A failing joint is in conflict when the retry left more than this fraction of its failure (residual over tolerance, or range broken over angle tolerance): a conflict is what more passes do not fix.

DFLT_MAX_DROP_TRIALS

The most trial solves solve() spends looking for the joint to give up in a conflict (each is a full solve of the frame).

DFLT_NUDGE

Radians each free body is turned by on a retry, alternating in sign (see _nudge): far below what a frame shows, enough to buckle a straight chain pushed along its length.

DFLT_POSITION_QUANTUM

finer than any render shows.

DFLT_SEGMENT_MARGIN

A contact or slide looks this far past a segment's ends (scene units) before letting the point go: a foot half a unit past the edge still stands.

SOLVER_VERSION

Bumped whenever the solve can give a different answer for the same inputs (a changed projection, order, or default): part of every cache key built on this module.

POSE_CHANNELS

Targets

an.kinematics.solver.solve(bodies, joints, *, targets=None, forces=(), gravity=None, dt=None, iterations=8, substeps=4, damping=0.02, tolerance=0.25, angle_tolerance=0.001, seed_chains=True, retry=4, drop_conflicts=True, seed=0)[source]#

One frame: the poses of all bodies that satisfy the joined joints with the targets held, forces and gravity acting.

dt=None is pose mode: no inertia, no gravity, no pushes (a gravity or a Force.vector is refused there: they need time; a Force.toward pull works in both modes). With a dt the bodies step through substeps of dt / substeps, carrying their velocities in and out, and a target is reached over those substeps. targets hold channels exactly (a body held in x and y is immovable to the joints for the frame, one held in rotation unturnable); a target beyond a chain’s reach shows up in unsatisfied rather than stretching anything. Hinge and weld rests left None are the relative angles the bodies have on input. A frame that leaves a joint failing is solved again with retry times the iterations (1: never); drop_conflicts then gives up the joints in conflict, last declared first (module docstring); False keeps them all and reports what fails. seed is reserved for stochastic drivers and does nothing in this version: the solve has no randomness.

A shoulder driven to a point, an upper arm pinned to it, a forearm hinged to that, the hand pinned to a handle (the two-link case): the hand lands on the handle and the elbow bends the way its range allows.

>>> torso = Body("torso", width=40.0, height=80.0)
>>> upper = Body("upper", x=20.0, y=-30.0, width=50.0, height=10.0, origin=(0.0, 0.5))
>>> fore = Body("fore", x=70.0, y=-30.0, width=40.0, height=8.0, origin=(0.0, 0.5))
>>> handle = Body("handle", x=80.0, y=10.0, width=4.0, height=4.0, fixed=True)
>>> joints = [Pin(Point("upper", 0.0, 0.5), Point("torso", 1.0, 0.125)),
...           Hinge(Point("fore", 0.0, 0.5), Point("upper", 1.0, 0.5), limits=(-2.6, 0.0)),
...           Pin(Point("fore", 1.0, 0.5), Point("handle", 0.5, 0.5))]
>>> r = solve([torso, upper, fore, handle], joints, targets={"torso": {"x": 0.0, "y": 0.0}})
>>> r.ok, tuple(round(v, 3) for v in r.residuals)
(True, (0.0, 0.0, 0.0))

Out of reach, the grip is reported, and the arm stays on its shoulder:

>>> far = handle.posed(x=300.0)
>>> r = solve([torso, upper, fore, far], joints, targets={"torso": {"x": 0.0, "y": 0.0}})
>>> [j for j, _ in r.unsatisfied], r.dropped, [round(v, 6) for v in r.residuals[:2]]
([2], (2,), [0.0, 0.0])
Return type:

SolveResult

an.kinematics.solver.solve_frames(bodies, joints, *, frames, dt, targets_at=None, joints_at=None, forces=(), gravity=None, **options)[source]#

frames solves in a row, each starting from the last: the caller’s time dimension. targets_at(k) (or targets_at[k]) gives frame k’s targets; joints_at(k) its joints (the same list with joined flags flipped is how a joint is unjoined and rejoined). Hinge and weld rests are fixed from the starting poses. options go to solve().

A part unjoined falls, and is caught where it is when rejoined:

>>> peg = Body("peg", width=2.0, height=2.0, fixed=True)
>>> part = Body("part", y=10.0, width=4.0, height=20.0, origin=(0.5, 0.0))
>>> joined = [Pin(Point("part", 0.5, 0.0), Point("peg", 0.5, 0.5))]
>>> loose = [Pin(Point("part", 0.5, 0.0), Point("peg", 0.5, 0.5), joined=False)]
>>> r = solve_frames([peg, part], joined, frames=12, dt=1 / 24, gravity=(0.0, 980.0),
...                  joints_at=lambda k: loose if 2 <= k < 8 else joined)
>>> bool(r.poses[1, 1, 1] < r.poses[7, 1, 1]), r.snaps[0][:2], r.snaps[0][2] > 2.0
(True, (8, 0), True)
>>> r.unsatisfied[-1]
