an.stage.hold#

The hold layer: what carries each item, frame by frame, and the hands that move it (an#502, catalogue A4).

The verbs are an.ir.holds (grasp, place, release, handover, swap). This module runs them inside the solve compile pass (an.stage.solve), because a hold needs the SOLVED hand at every frame: the hand is a body of the shot’s puppet, the item is not.

The model. Every item is, at each instant, in exactly one *holder’s frame*: a body’s point (a hand, a figurine’s socket), the ground, a point of the stage, or none (in flight). A change of holder is a reframe that keeps the item’s world pose at that instant (keep: world, an#502): the item’s local transform in the new holder’s frame is computed so the picture is continuous, so there is never a slide or a jump. What makes the change look handled is that the hand first goes where the item is:

  • Reach and retract. A gesture drives its hand’s point (an end effector: stu@hand_r, stu/arm_r) along a minimum-jerk path from where the shot would have it to the goal (the item’s grip; for a place, the point that puts the item’s contact point on its target), and back, as a pull row of the solver whose goal moves frame by frame. The arm follows by the solver’s joints. The item changes holder at the frame the hand arrives.

  • Lean. When the goal is out of the arm’s reach, the body the arm hangs from (the chain’s root: a torso, a crane’s base) turns about its own pivot by the least angle that brings the goal in reach (closed form, the two-link triangle), up to lean degrees, rising with the reach and falling with the retract, and every part riding on it turns with it (the head, the other arm). A reach that still cannot arrive is reported (unreachable_hold, with how far it stays short and which way to move the carrier).

  • Carry. A held item’s world transform is its holder’s times a fixed local transform, so it turns, mirrors and squashes with the hand (a character turning round carries the musket round with it).

  • Place. Over a place’s reach the item keeps its point in the hand and turns from how it is carried to how it will rest (a wrist), so it arrives aligned with a socket’s frame or lying on the ground at its rest angle, its lowest INK point at floor height (the texture’s alpha, not its box, so a carved part with a transparent margin does not float).

  • Release. A parabola under gravity (the shot’s or the style pack’s, else DFLT_FALL_GRAVITY) from the velocity the hand gave it, turning to its

    rest angle on the way down, landing on the ground exactly; onto: ground@x solves the throw that lands there on an arc arc px high.

Draw order. While a body holds an item, the item is PAINTED just after its holder’s part (an#537): a paint_after channel on the item’s entity node (an.base.PAINT_AFTER_PROPERTY) names that part from the frame the hold changes, and "" (its staged place, stage.after included) once nothing holds it. The item keeps its own node, so its transform is untouched; the runtime paints it from right after the part (runtime.js, makeHeldPaints). A musket in a hand draws over the arm that carries it and under whatever the holder draws over that arm; put in a figurine’s hand, it draws with the figurine, not over the one who put it there.

Two passes. The goals and the leans are planned from a solve without the gestures (the shot as authored), the shot is solved with them, and the plan is made again from that solve (an item carried after a grasp hangs where the grasp really put it) before the final solve. HOLD_PLAN_PASSES says how many times.

The item’s channels (x, y, rotation, scale_x, scale_y of its entity node) are emitted with the puppet’s in the entity’s __solve__ clip; meta.extensions.solve.holds records each gesture (its contact instant, where the hand arrived, how far it stayed short, its lean) and each item’s holders, what an validate’s hold checks read (an.verify.holds).

an.stage.hold.paint_hosts(holders, times, *, lead=1e-06)[source]#

The keys of an item’s paint_after channel (an#537) from its holders [(frame, holder)] in the order they changed: the part it paints just after ("": its staged place, when no body holds it), at each change; at one frame the last change stands. A change key leads its frame by lead seconds, so the frame sees it however its time was rounded (the property is discrete(switch_at=1): it switches at its own key; a space that declares it otherwise must keep that). [] when no body ever holds it: nothing to emit, and the shot compiles as before.

>>> hand = Holder("body", ref="fig/arm_r", body="fig/arm_r", local=(0.0, 0.0))
>>> times = [0.0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0]
>>> paint_hosts([(0, Holder("ground")), (2, hand), (4, None)], times, lead=0.25)
[(0.0, ''), (0.75, 'fig/arm_r'), (1.75, '')]
