an.verify.constraints#

Constraints: declarative statements a lint checks and a solver may enforce (an#497).

A constraint says something about the world the shots show that must stay true: a node does not jump within a shot, two solids do not pass through each other, a prop meets the body it is drawn on. It is declared once and has up to two faces (the constraints catalogue, misc/docs/constraints_catalogue.md):

  • a check: a lint, run by an validate through the semantic-check registry (an.genres.SemanticCheck). It reads a an.ir.validate.ValidationContext and adds findings to ctx.report;

  • a term, optional: the residual the kinematic layer’s solver drives to zero, for a constraint that is enforced as well as checked. None means lint-only, and that is the legitimate default rather than a stub: draw order is derived from depth and continuity comes from solving each frame from the previous one, so neither has a residual.

Every constraint ships as a lint before it is a term, and a term never replaces the lint: what the solver produces is checked again by the same check.

The one example a genre or a later batch copies (P2’s joint_violation):

>>> def joint_check(ctx):
...     "Every joint of ctx.shot holds at every sampled instant, else ctx.report.add(...)."
>>> def joint_term(state):
...     "The solver's residual for one joint: zero when its two bodies meet there."
...     return 0.0
>>> JOINT = Constraint("joint_violation", joint_check, term=joint_term,
...                    description="a joint's two bodies meet at the joint")
>>> check = JOINT.semantic_check()
>>> check.name, check.stage, check.order == DFLT_ORDER, JOINT.term is joint_term
('joint_violation', 'shot', True, True)

A genre registers it like any check (Genre(checks=(JOINT.semantic_check(),))); the core’s own are CONSTRAINTS, registered when this module is imported (an.ir imports it, so validate_semantic always has them, the way the core’s other checks are registered when an.ir.validate is).

The core’s constraints:

  • continuity (an#508, catalogue B1): a node does not jump within a shot unless a set says so, and a value is not held for less than a frame. discontinuities() is the pure function under it.

  • solidity (an#509, catalogue A2): two solids (characters and props) whose boxes overlap, with no stage.after between them, are drawn through each other. solid_overlaps() is the pure function under it.

  • draw_order (an#510, catalogue A3): two entities painted one way in one shot and the other way in a later one, with no change of stage.after between them. order_flips() is the pure function under it. (The within-shot half, painted order against world depth for a member in motion, is the lowering’s: an.ir.world.depth_order_problems, reported as a draw_order note.)

  • jump_cut (catalogue C4, the 30-degree rule): two consecutive shots of one subject from nearly the same angle and size. jump_cut_between() is the pure function under it.

  • object_permanence (catalogue C1): an entity shown, then absent for whole shots, then shown again with no time_jump between and no entrance; and one entity drawn twice in a shot. vanishings() is the pure function under it.

  • held_item (catalogue A4, the weld): an item declared held by a node (Shot.welds) stays at its offset from the carrier between pick-up and release. weld_drifts() is the pure function under it.

  • rigidity (catalogue A5): an entity that declares overrides: {rigid: true} keeps its aspect (scale_x against scale_y) through the shot; one that declares overrides: {preserve_volume: true} keeps scale_x * scale_y. shape_drifts() is the pure function under it. Opt-in, since the

    squash_stretch preset deforms on purpose.

  • motion_style (catalogue B2, B5): the style pack’s motion targets, measured on the compiled shot: smoothness (the log dimensionless jerk of each move) and anticipation (a big move is preceded by a small counter-move). move_jerk() and unanticipated() are the pure functions under it.

    Silent when the pack declares no motion. Arcs and overlap are not built.

  • gravity (catalogue B4): under a style pack that declares gravity, an entity declaring overrides: {airborne: {from, until}} follows a parabola (constant downward acceleration) and does not hover. airborne_faults() is the pure function under it. Unsupported falls and hangs wait on supports (catalogue A1) and joints.

  • causality (catalogue B7): a shot sound cue that names its visible cause (a CueAnchor) is not ahead of it (error past CAUSE_TOLERANCE) and sits within the audio-visual sync window

    (warning). av_offset_fault() is the pure function under it. Dialogue and lip-sync consistency are not built.

an.verify.constraints.check_not_compiled(ctx)[source]#

Say, once per shot, that the compile-based checks did NOT run on a shot that does not compile, at an.verify.vision.FAILURE_SEVERITY: a check that could not look must never read as a check that passed (an#551).

Runs at finish over the compiles the shot checks asked for (the memo under COMPILED_KEY), so it says nothing when no compile-based check ran. Silent for a shot with an entity whose store was not supplied (checks not asked for are not broken). The cause is given in full unless a finding on the shot already says it (_already_said()), then “reported above”. Folds in the action-target check’s own skip.

>>> from an.ir.schema import SceneIR, Shot
>>> from an.ir.validate import ValidationContext, ValidationReport
>>> ctx = ValidationContext(SceneIR(timeline=[Shot(id="a"), Shot(id="b")]), ValidationReport(), {})
>>> ctx.memo[(COMPILED_KEY, 0)] = (None, ValueError("step_hz 1000 is not a divisor of fps"))
>>> ctx.memo[(COMPILED_KEY, 1)] = (None, ValueError("step_hz 1000 is not a divisor of fps"))
>>> ctx.report.add("error", "timeline/1/step_hz", "step_hz 1000 is not a divisor of fps.")
>>> check_not_compiled(ctx)
>>> [(f.severity, f.ir_path) for f in ctx.report.findings[1:]]
[('warning', 'timeline/0'), ('warning', 'timeline/1')]
>>> [f.description.split("(")[-1] for f in ctx.report.findings[1:]]
['ValueError: step_hz 1000 is not a divisor of fps).', 'ValueError, reported above).']

an.verify.constraints.discontinuities(timeline, *, rest, duration)[source]#

Every instant in (0, duration] where a numeric value of timeline jumps: the pose read just before the instant and just after it differ beyond CONTINUITY_TOLERANCE. rest is each (target, property)’s value while nothing writes it (the node’s own transform); a key absent from both the pose and rest is not judged.

A tween with a from the node is not at jumps where it starts; two tweens on one property jump where the later one starts and again where it ends and the earlier one shows through; a loop whose last pose is not its first jumps at each restart. A set is a jump the author wrote: it is reported with deliberate=True, for the caller to leave alone.

>>> from an.timing.channel import Channel, Keyframe
>>> from an.timing.clip import Clip
>>> from an.timing.timeline import PlacedClip, Timeline, Track
>>> rise = Clip("rise", 1.0, [Channel("a", "y", [Keyframe(0.0, 3.0, "linear"), Keyframe(1.0, 10.0)])])
>>> tl = Timeline(2.0, [Track("a", [PlacedClip(rise, start_time=0.5)])])
>>> (d,) = discontinuities(tl, rest={("a", "y"): 0.0}, duration=2.0)
>>> d.at, d.before, d.after, d.cause, d.deliberate
(0.5, 0.0, 3.0, 'start', False)
>>> hold = Clip("hold", 1.5, [Channel("a", "y", [Keyframe(0.0, 3.0, "step")])])
>>> tl = Timeline(2.0, [Track("a", [PlacedClip(hold, start_time=0.5), PlacedClip(rise, start_time=0.5)])])
>>> discontinuities(tl, rest={("a", "y"): 0.0}, duration=2.0)[0].deliberate
True

list[Discontinuity]

an.verify.constraints.move_jerk(values, dt)[source]#

The log dimensionless jerk ln(T^5 / A^2 * integral |jerk|^2 dt) of a move sampled every dt s: lower is smoother. A minimum-jerk move scores about ln 720 (6.6) whatever its size and duration; corners score far higher. None for a move too short or too small to measure.

>>> import math
>>> s = [10 * u**3 - 15 * u**4 + 6 * u**5 for u in (i / 60 for i in range(61))]
>>> 6.0 < move_jerk(s, 1 / 30) < 7.0
True
>>> linear = [i / 60 for i in range(61)]
>>> move_jerk(linear, 1 / 30) > 9
True

an.verify.constraints.vanishings(presence, time_jumps, entrances=None)[source]#

The entities that vanish and return with nothing to explain it.

presence[i] is the ids drawn in shot i; time_jumps[i] says the transition into shot i passes time; entrances[i] is the ids shot i opens by acting on. A return after absent shots is explained by a time jump into any shot from the first absent one to the returning one, or by an entrance.

>>> shots = [frozenset({"a"}), frozenset(), frozenset({"a"})]
>>> vanishings(shots, [False] * 3)
[Vanishing(entity='a', before=0, after=2)]
>>> vanishings(shots, [False, False, True])
[]
>>> vanishings(shots, [False] * 3, [frozenset(), frozenset(), frozenset({"a"})])

>>> vanishings([frozenset({"a"}), frozenset({"a"})], [False] * 2)
