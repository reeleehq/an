# ADR 0007 — A scene space: one world, and shots as viewpoints onto it

**Status:** Proposed, 2026-10-20 · **Decider:** the maintainer · **Related:** an#484 (the travelling view, re-based on this), an#478 (the dolly: a consumer), an#445/an#467 (`screen_position`, `node_box`: what the extents are measured with), an#394 (cut continuity: amended by this), an#257 (previz: the view-space vocabulary), reeleehq/cutan `body-attachment-lint` (attachments in the character's units, placed from the character's `stage`), ADR 0001 (core and genre), ADR 0003 (structured ↔ semantic), the research note *A scene space for an: one world, many shots* (2026-10-20, in the production's working folder and the owner's annals)

## Context

A shot's entities are placed in **screen space**, per shot: `AssetRef.stage` is `at: (x, y)` in scene pixels from the stage centre, a `scale`, and a draw anchor `after`. Nothing relates one shot's placements to another's. A production (a car scene: a two-shot through the windscreen and close singles of each man) therefore placed every shot by hand, and the close-ups did not follow from the two-shot: the seat beside Paul was empty in his single, the steering wheel changed size relative to him between framings, and the receding road was 750 hand-eased screen-space actions that had to be re-mapped by script when a framing moved. The owner's request: *a model of where things are and the world they take, possibly in 3D though the rendering is 2D; shots of one scene are consistent with it; close-ups keep the objects and distances; this is a minimum core of any animation system.* The refinement after a second render (an#484): *perspective from the world model, not hand-eased screen moves; streams keep phase across cuts; plates for a moving view hold only what is at infinity; don't solve for this video, solve it for all future creations.*

The research (see Related) finds one architecture under thirty years of virtual cinematography, every 2D tool that supports shots of one scene (Harmony, Moho, Animate, Cartoon Animator, Grease Pencil), and the current generative-video work: an explicit scene with depth, and cameras computed from compositional intent, lowered per engine. Film continuity (the line, eyelines, coverage from a master) is a property of that model, not of a shot.

What the core has: `screen_position`/`node_box` to measure a built entity without a browser; a 2D camera (`root.pivot`); planes with a parallax factor; a cut-continuity check that assumes a placement carries across a cut; `an.ir.crowd` as the pattern for authoring sugar that lowers to ordinary entities and actions.

## Decision

1. **A scene has at most one `world`** (the key is `world`, not `space`: `space` already names a node's property space in the genre registry) (`SceneIR.world`, opt-in, omitted when unset: no stored scene changes). It is a list of **members**, each the same `{kind, id, store, ref}` reference a shot's entity carries, at a world position `at: [x, y, z]` with a size (`height` or `width` in world units; `units_per_px` as the fallback for art whose extent cannot be measured). The world's axes are the stage's extended with depth: `x` right, `y` down, `z` away from the viewer. The unit is the author's (metres recommended). A member may instead be **attached** to another (`attached: {to, at?, scale?}`): it has no world position of its own; with an offset it is derived from the host's placement in the host's native units, without one it is added whenever the host is and left to the genre to place (the cut-out genre's body attachments). A member carries `after` (the draw anchor), never `stage`.

2. **A shot is a viewpoint onto the world** (`Shot.viewpoint`, opt-in, omitted when unset). A viewpoint is a pinhole camera: `at` (world), `look: [yaw, pitch, roll]` in degrees (yaw turns right, pitch looks down, roll turns the picture clockwise), or `look_at: [x, y, z]`, or `vanish: [sx, sy]` (where the `+z` direction lands on screen: the road's vanishing point), and `fov` (horizontal, degrees; default `DFLT_FOV_DEG`) or `focal` (pixels). Or a **composition**: `subject: <member or list>`, `size: close_up | medium | full | wide` (the frame cuts the subject at the conventional landmark), `subject_at: [sx, sy]` (where the subject lands on screen), from which the camera is computed. The world may hold **named viewpoints** (`world.viewpoints`) and a shot names one: the coverage of a scene (two-shot, Jerry's single, Paul's single) is written once and reused by every shot that cuts back to it. `hide` leaves members out of a shot, unless the shot lists them: the listing is the more specific statement and wins, with an info note.

3. **Lowering, not a new renderer.** A viewpoint derives each member's `stage` placement, `at = f·(p − c)/depth`, `scale = size/native × f/depth`, in the IR layer (`an.ir.world.lower_world`) before any compile pass. Members in frame are added to the shot; a listed member without `stage.at` is filled in; a hand `stage.at` **always wins** (a `stage.scale` of exactly 1.0, the field's default, means the world's scale, so a nudge keeps the member's size and what is attached to it follows the nudge); draw order is depth (far first), environments first, listed non-members on top. An attached member the world adds carries its attachment on the entity (`overrides.attached: {to, at?, scale}`), so a genre pass that places it from its host finds the host in the shot. A lowered scene lowered again gains nothing. The compiled document, the shot-cache key (which digests the compiled document) and every renderer are unchanged; the 2D camera remains what it is, a move within the framed picture. The lowered scene is what `an render`, `an preview`, `an probe`, `an validate` and the orchestrator compile; it is never written back to the project.

4. **The travelling view is the viewpoint moving through the world in film time** (`world.travel`: `direction`, `speed`, `near`, `far`, `streams`, `plates`). Members ride with the viewpoints (they are on the vehicle); streams are rows of props positioned in the world along the direction (`every`, `lane`, `y`, seeded), lowered to `set`/`tween` actions with cubic-Bézier easings fitted to the projection (split where the fit misses `tolerance_px`: dense where `1/z` bends), cut to each instance's on-screen window. Entities are **slots**, one per instance that can be between `near` and `far` at once, each drawing the instances that pass through it one after another, so the entity count is bounded by the depth range and never by the shot's length; the same world instance lands in the same slot in every shot. A shot whose travel would lower to more than `max_actions` is refused with the knobs named. Phase holds across cuts because instances are positioned by film time, not per shot; a side window is the same projection under a yawed viewpoint. `plates` names, for every plane under a travel, whether it is `far` (at infinity: the sky, the horizon; parallax factor must be 0) or `vehicle` (rigid with the camera); a plane in neither list is a warning, and the art guidance says a far plate holds nothing nearer than the far range.

5. **Continuity becomes computable.** `an validate` gains: `world` (members, references, sizes), `viewpoint` (a hand placement that disagrees with the world; a listed entity the frame does not show; members the world adds, as info; the travel's plates), `screen_direction` (two entities present across a cut whose left/right order flips: the camera crossed the line), and `cut_continuity` no longer compares placements across two different viewpoints (a cut between framings is a cut, not a jump).

6. **Genre specifics stay in the genre.** The core measures a member's native extent by compiling its specimen and taking the union of its nodes' boxes (`an.stage.world.member_extents`, any registered kind, no browser). Body attachments, views (a reverse angle needs the character's `back` view), faces and gaze are the cut-out genre's; an eyeline lint and view selection from the viewpoint are filed there. Shot-size landmarks are a core table on human proportions (`SHOT_SIZES`); a genre may later register landmarks from its rig (a seam, not built).

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep screen-space placement and add a scene-level "re-fit" command** (the production's `rewire_scene.py` as a tool): fixes one video; every new framing is a new hand placement and the lints stay impossible. Rejected by the owner's words.
- **Frame with the 2D camera** (a close-up as `zoom` on the master's stage): a zoom scales the environment planes with the cast, so a plate drawn for the two-shot shows its edge or pixelates in the close-up, and a close-up usually has its own plate; it also spends the 2D camera, which stays free for moves within the framed picture. The pinhole on billboards gives the same picture for the cast and leaves the plates per shot.
- **A full 3D scene graph** (rotate the billboards, light them, occlude): the stage is a 2D engine and the art is 2D; projecting anchors and sizes is exactly the multiplane model every 2D tool ships. A planar member at depth is the right primitive; a plane in the world (a plate at a world depth, giving parallax from the projection rather than a factor) is the next add, at this seam.
- **Lower in the compiler** (a compile pass): the world is scene-level and the phase is film-level; a shot compiles alone. The IR-level lowering keeps `compile_shot(shot)` what it is and lets a genre's compile passes (body placement at order 90) read the derived `stage` as if the author wrote it.
- **Persist the derived placements** (`an space apply` writing `stage` into `scene.md`): do-then-redo; the author would edit the world and the stale placements would win. The derived form is shown (`an world show`), never stored.

## Consequences

- An author describes the scene once and frames it per shot; a close-up of Paul shows Jerry beside him because the world says so. Coverage is named and reused. The art guidance changes: plates are per viewpoint and, under a travel, hold only what is far.
- `an validate` can say *the camera crossed the line* and *the world puts Jerry here, the shot put him there*. IR paths in those findings name the author's entity index, or `entities/~<id>` for a member the world added (it has no line in `scene.md` to patch); `an world show` prints the lowered shot.
- Motion actions stay in shot screen space (a 2 px seat bob is 2 px in any framing). World-space motion (a member walking in world units, tweens relative to a derived placement) is filed, not built.
- The shot-cache key moves for a shot whose world placement changes, through the compiled document, and for no other shot.
- A genre pass that places from `stage` (body attachments) runs after the lowering by construction.

Built in this ADR's PR: the schema (`Space`, `WorldMember`, `Attachment`, `Viewpoint`, `Travel`, `Stream`), the projection and composition resolvers, `lower_world` with streams, `member_extents`, the `scene.md` blocks (`yaml world`, `viewpoint:` in `yaml shot`), the four validate changes, `an world show`, the entry-point hooks, tests and doctests. Not built, filed as issues: world-space motion and relative tweens; a plane at a world depth (true parallax and the dolly, an#478); a member fixed in the world under a travel (a landmark passing by); `an space from-shot` (derive a world from a hand-placed master); landmarks from a rig (`SHOT_SIZES` seam) and automatic view selection and the eyeline lint (cutan); a content check of far plates by a vision model (cutan#95); previz sharing the viewpoint vocabulary (an#257).
