# ADR 0008 — A constraint layer under structured animation: bodies, joints, drivers and frames, solved per frame

**Status:** Proposed, 2026-10-21 · **Decider:** the maintainer · **Related:** [an#497](https://github.com/reeleehq/an/issues/497) (the epic: plan, package split, the owner's decisions), ADR 0007 (the scene space this extends; amended in one consequence), an#484 (the travelling view), an#478 (the dolly: a consumer of a frame at a depth), an#340 (nested bone chains: forward kinematics, the tree the joints are read from), an#344 (`StagePlacement.after`: the draw-order mechanism), an#394 (cut continuity: generalised by the size-conservation check), an#489 (the affine re-fit: superseded), reeleehq/cutan#101 (body attachment: the first joints, under another name), reeleehq/cutan#97 (two-bone IK arms: the solver's first consumer), reeleehq/cutan#98 (socket drift through motion: a check on this layer), ADR 0001 (core and genre), ADR 0003 (structured ↔ semantic), ADR 0004 (every stage a pure function of digests), ADR 0006 (contracts authored in `an`)

## Context

Structured animation is AI translating instructions into sequences of parametric commands that puppeteer elements to narrate something. Image generation is optional asset sourcing; video generation plays no part. What keeps a sequence of such commands believable is consistency: a hand stays on the wheel it grips, a lamp rests on its table, a man is the same size in his close-up as in the two-shot, the trees outside the window move while the dashboard does not. Each of these has so far been fixed where it was noticed: a prop placed from the shoulders at the start of a shot (reeleehq/cutan#101), close singles derived from the two-shot by script, a plate rule for moving windows (reeleehq/cutan#99), a world model for the travelling view (ADR 0007). The owner's instruction, in intent: *consistency is one general problem, whether about parts of a body, object placement or movement; look for solutions that are principled and general, not about a character or a human body but about how the world works. Make puppeteering a category of cut-out animation in the strict sense: elements grouped as solid shapes joined at explicit points, which constrain how the pieces move; the frames we create cannot violate those constraints unless a joint is explicitly unjoined; optional gravity pulls elements down; invisible or visible strings and rods pull and push. More generally, define sequences of positions for some elements and have the others resolve according to the joins, gravity settling among the options. Catalogue the other structural constraints that force consistency, and generalise inside-the-car versus outside versus far background into planes of movement.*

What the core has. Rigs are bone trees (`Bone.parent`; `nesting: bones` gives forward kinematics to any depth, an#340) with no joint limits, bone lengths or inverse kinematics; a bone's `pivot` names the artist's joint circle and is used once, at authoring. Animation channels replace values (an authored `rotation` is the final rotation, nothing adds to it); `evaluate_timeline` is a pure function of time, parity-tested against the browser runtime; the `camera_follow` pass already evaluates the partial document at every frame and emits one key per frame, which is the per-frame hook a solver needs. The scene space (ADR 0007) gives members a world position and a viewpoint per shot, lowers them to static placements before compile, and keeps motion in shot screen space. Attachment exists under three spellings that nothing relates: `Bone.parent` in a rig, `attached: {to}` on a space member, and `metadata.body_attachment` sockets on a cut-out prop, the last placed once per shot from rest bones and never re-solved while the arm moves. There is no cross-entity parenting in the scene graph, no notion of a force, and no shared interface between the checks that exist (`body_attachment_consistency`, `cut_continuity`, `screen_direction`).

The research behind this record (puppetry traditions and puppet-like animation; the kinematics of rigid bodies with pin and hinge joints, IK, position-based dynamics; the catalogue of structural constraints; nested frames of reference and the multiplane camera) is linked from an#497 as it lands. Two findings fix the shape here: every puppetry tradition is bodies joined at points, driven at a few points by strings, rods or hands, with gravity settling the rest; and every 2D tool that keeps shots consistent does so with a scene graph of frames whose motion composes down the tree.

## Decision

1. **One layer, four nouns and one verb, in `an`'s IR, additive and opt-in.** A scene that declares no joint, driver or frame compiles byte-for-byte as today: the new pass is a no-op and no stored document changes.

2. **A body is a unit of rigidity.** Any node with a transform is a body: an entity, a part or bone node of a rig, a plane, a space member. Bodies are references to existing nodes, not a new entity kind; a body may declare `fixed` (it never moves under the solver) and an extent (otherwise measured by `node_box`/`member_extents`). Rigidity and size conservation are properties of a body: a body's parts keep their relative geometry and a body keeps its size across shots unless a declaration says otherwise.

3. **A joint is a declared relation between two bodies, or a body and a frame.** `joints:` on a shot (for the shot) or on the scene (for the production): `{type, a: <body>[@point], b: <body>[@point] | <frame>, limits?, from?, to?}`; a point is given in the body's own units (a fraction of its extent, a bone, or a named landmark such as a grip point). Types: `pin` (coincident points), `hinge` (a pin with an angle range), `slide` (a point on a segment), `weld` (fixed relative pose), `distance` (a rope or rod: equal, at most, at least), `contact` (one-sided: a point on or above a surface). A joint is joined over its span and may be unjoined and rejoined in time by the `unjoin` and `join` action kinds; a frame may not violate a joined joint. **A rig's bones are joints**: a `nesting: bones` rig declares a pin joint at every bone origin between child and parent, so the artist's pivots stop being discarded; a genre registers limits per part type through the existing registries. **`attached: {to}` on a space member is a weld** and stays as sugar. **`metadata.body_attachment` sockets are pin joints** between a prop's socket points and a character's bones, and keep their persisted shape.

4. **A driver is what moves bodies, and the existing vocabulary already is one.** Any `set`, `tween` or `play` channel on a body is a position target: the solver meets it exactly where the joints allow and moves only the undriven bodies to satisfy the joints. Two new drivers are action kinds: `force` (a rod or string: a vector, or a pull towards a point, applied at a point of a body, with a strength) and gravity, declared per shot or per style (`physics: {gravity, damping}`), optional and off by default. Gravity settles the degrees of freedom nothing else determines; it is a tie-breaker, not a simulation.

5. **A frame is a frame of reference with a parent and a motion.** `frames:` in the scene space: `{name, parent, motion?}`; a body or member carries `frame:` (default: the shot's screen, so nothing existing changes). The world is the root frame; the camera is a frame; a vehicle interior is a frame in the world; a plane with a parallax factor is the lowering of a frame at a depth; a moving platform or a camera rig is a frame with a driven motion. Motion composes down the chain: body in frame, parent frames, world, then ADR 0007's `Projection`, then the stage. A body changes frame at an event (`reframe`: it leaves one frame for another at a time, keeping its world pose, as a ball thrown from a window). This amends ADR 0007's consequence that motion stays in shot screen space: **motion may be declared in any frame and is lowered to screen space at compile**; declared in no frame it is screen space as before.

6. **Solve is a compile pass in `an`, deterministic, per frame.** `solve` runs between `face` (order 500) and `camera_follow` (550): it assembles the partial document, evaluates the authored channels at every frame, solves the joined joints under the active drivers and limits, and emits dense keys for the undriven bodies' channels, honouring `step_hz` as the camera does (its own emission site). The solver is `an.kinematics`, a pure-numpy module that imports nothing from the IR (bodies as arrays, joints as rows), reached through one `solver=` keyword whose default is the in-house implementation; the method (position-based dynamics with a chain solver such as FABRIK or CCD for limbs) is fixed by the measured comparison in the epic's first implementation issue. Determinism is a requirement, not a goal: a fixed iteration count, a fixed constraint order, seeds for anything stochastic, bitwise-equal output across runs. The iteration count and the solver's version are render knobs in the shot-cache key; the solved keys land in the compiled document, so the key covers them.

7. **One interface for every constraint: a lint first, a solver term when it has one.** A `Constraint` is a declarative statement with `check` (a `SemanticCheck` over the compiled or lowered output, reporting `Finding`s at a tolerance) and an optional `term` (a residual the solver minimises). Every joint type is a constraint whose check says where and when the compiled output violates it. The catalogue of structural constraints (contact and support, non-penetration, size conservation across shots, draw order and occlusion consistency, object permanence across cuts, screen direction and eyelines, timing as a style term) ships on this interface, each as a lint before any solver term. `body_attachment_consistency`, `cut_continuity` and `screen_direction` are re-expressed on it without changing their names.

8. **Core and genre, by ADR 0001's test.** `an` owns decisions 1 to 7: a data-visualisation genre needs bodies, joints, frames and a solver without saying character, rig or a style's name. `cutan` owns: deriving joints and limits from its rigs; the arm split (upper arm, forearm, hand) and grip points on props (reeleehq/cutan#97 becomes the first consumer of the solver, not a bespoke two-bone IK); the lowering of `body_attachment` sockets to pin joints; **puppet styles** as style specs that declare joints, strings (distance constraints from a control-bar body), rods and gravity: marionette, rod puppet, shadow puppet (hinge-only planar figures), bunraku; the motion presets re-expressed as drivers; the art guidance. **No new package in v1**: `an.kinematics` is marked as an extraction candidate and becomes its own package when a second consumer exists.

## Alternatives considered

- **Per-feature fixes** (a two-bone IK for arms in `cutan`, a placement rule per prop, a plate rule per view): each solves one scene and the next one needs another rule; the owner's words reject this.
- **A physics engine as a dependency** (pymunk, Box2D, a game engine's solver): these simulate dynamics; we solve poses under targets, with gravity as a tie-breaker. Their floating-point behaviour varies across platforms and versions, which breaks the byte-sensitive contract hashes and the shot cache (ADR 0004). The `solver=` seam leaves the door open for a specific case.
- **Solve in the runtime at playback** (the browser evaluates joints): the compiled document is the contract and the cache key; Python and JavaScript would each need a solver and a parity suite twice the size of the timing kernel's.
- **Solve at IR lowering**, like `lower_space`: the lowering is static per shot; the solve is per frame and needs the compiled clips (face, play expansions). Lowering stays where ADR 0007 put it; time is the compiler's.
- **Full rigid-body dynamics** (mass, momentum, collisions): puppetry is kinematics plus a little gravity; momentum and arcs are timing constraints and ship as lints in the catalogue. Dynamics is a later term on the same interface, if ever.
- **A new package for the solver now**: no second consumer exists; architecture-first says a module with an extraction note.

## Consequences

- An author writes *pin the hand to the handle, nod the torso*, and the arm follows; a steering wheel is two pin joints and a view, placed and kept in every framing and at every frame, which retires the per-shot hand placement of reeleehq/cutan#101 while keeping its declaration.
- `an validate` can say *the hand left the handle at 1.2 s*, *the lamp floats 12 px above the table*, *the body shrank 8% between shots*, *the ball is still in the car's frame after it left the window*.
- Compile cost rises by one dense per-frame solve for shots that declare joints or frames, bounded by the fixed iteration count and cached by digest; shots without them pay nothing.
- ADR 0007 is amended in one consequence (motion in any frame) and extended at the seams it named (`Projection`, `attached`, a plane at a world depth, a member fixed in the world under a travel). an#489 is superseded.
- `cutan`'s persisted identifiers do not change; `join`, `unjoin`, `force` and `reframe` are additive action kinds; a scene without them is unchanged.
- The style spec gains a place to say what a puppet is (joints, strings, gravity), so *in the style of a marionette* is a declaration the solver and the lints enforce, not a look the art imitates.

## First slice

Built in the epic's first three issues (an#497 P1–P3): `an.kinematics` with pin, hinge, distance and weld joints, limits, position targets and gravity, deterministic, with doctests and a bitwise-determinism test; the joints and frames schema in the IR with `join`, `unjoin` and `force`; the `solve` pass with the byte-identity test for scenes that declare nothing and the `joint_violation` check; in `cutan`, joints derived from rigs, hand and forearm bones with grip points, `body_attachment` lowered to pin joints, and the marionette style as the first puppet style. Not built, filed under the epic: frames in full (a plane at a world depth and the dolly an#478, moving platforms, camera rigs, `reframe`), the constraint catalogue's lints beyond `joint_violation`, the remaining puppet styles, the vision-model critic (reeleehq/cutan#95), previz sharing the vocabulary (an#257).
