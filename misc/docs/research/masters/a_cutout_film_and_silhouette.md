# Study the masters, family A: cut-out film, silhouette, paper-cut and shadow puppetry

*2026-10-01 · part of [`../study_the_masters.md`](../study_the_masters.md) (an#223) · sources S01–S09*

Each entry gives what the source is, its **methods** (tagged with the method ids of the hub's taxonomy, §4), **measurable cues** where a source states them, what it **contributes** that the others do not, the **written analyses** (numbered references below), **video examples** (links only; nothing is downloaded or committed) and a **copyright** hint. Tags on numbers: **[L n]** stated by reference n; **[E]** estimate or inference. Video links marked *(search)* were seen in search results but not opened. No timestamp is given unless one was verified; for the six sources measured in `cutout_styles_research.md` the timestamp is the start of the measured window.

## S01 · Lotte Reiniger (`RE`) — silhouette film

**What.** German silhouette animator; *Die Abenteuer des Prinzen Achmed* (1926), the oldest surviving animated feature. Background and measured statistics: `cutout_styles_research.md` §4.5 [1].

**Methods.** Figures cut from black card and thin lead, every limb a separate piece joined with wire hinges, 20–50 pieces per puppet (`rig.hierarchy`, `surface.silhouette`) [2][3]. Lit from below on a glass table so hinges vanish; layered translucent paper backgrounds on several glass planes, an early multiplane (`stage.multiplane`) [2]. Figures kept to profile, the profile is the drawing (`swap.fixed_profile`, `stage.profile_strip`). Naturalistic, closely studied walks and flights (`loco.legged_cycle`) [2]. No mouth animation; expression is pose (`speech.pose_only`, `face.posture_only`). A locked camera in the measured clip (`camera.locked`) [1]. Shot counts computed from the score's musical values (`timing.music_count`, `sound.score_led`) [2]. Intertitles (`text.card`); dissolves between plates (`transition.dissolve`).

**Cues.** 24 fps (some sources say 18) [3]; 65 min, three years [3]; measured 4 cuts/min, mean shot 12 s, 93% one-frame change intervals on a transcode of unknown source rate [1].

**Contributes.** The deep articulated puppet, and timing derived from the music rather than the dialogue.

**Written.** [2] BFI, Reiniger in her own words; [4] Reiniger's book *Shadow Theatres and Shadow Films* (1970); [5] Grace, *Lotte Reiniger: Pioneer of Film Animation* (2017).

**Video.** [Prince Achmed, Wak-Wak excerpt](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C5SwrNtePmQ&t=0) (the measured window); Internet Archive prints: [German/English intertitles](https://archive.org/details/abenteuer-des-prinzen-achmed-1926-german-english), [another print](https://archive.org/details/die-abenteuer-des-prinzen-achmed).

**Copyright.** US public domain since 2022 (published 1926) [E]; in Germany Reiniger died in 1981, so likely protected to the end of 2051 [E]. The 1999 tinted restoration may carry its own rights. The best public-domain study candidate in this family, for US-based study only, after a check against the plan's legal guidance.

## S02 · Michel Ocelot (`OC`) — silhouette, from paper to CG to stereo

**What.** French director: *Ciné si* (TV, 1989), *Princes et Princesses* (2000), *Dragons et Princesses* (TV, 2010), *Les Contes de la nuit* (2011, stereo 3D) [6][7].

**Methods.** Paper era: black paper figures under the camera, backlit, figures moving independently of a set built on several levels (`surface.silhouette`, `stage.multiplane`) [6]. An "Egyptian" twisted perspective: torso frontal, head and legs in profile, staged side-on (`swap.fixed_profile`, `stage.profile_strip`) [6]. A silhouette shows no face, so expression and speech are carried by posture and profile (`face.posture_only`, `speech.pose_only`) [E]. A staging rule that follows from the black fill: a limb lost inside the body outline cannot be read, so every pose keeps limbs off the outline [6]. Colour only in the backlit, patterned backgrounds (`stage.flat_graphic`). The digital films render CG (Maya) as silhouettes; in stereo, depth is used to separate black-on-black overlaps and to make crowds readable (`rig.draw_order_keys`, `stage.crowd`) [6]. Animatic voiced by Ocelot, then real voices, which the animators follow (`sound.voice_first`) [6]. A frame story in which three people invent each tale (`speech.narrator`).

**Cues.** *Ciné si* 8 × 12 min; *Dragons et Princesses* 10 × 13 min with more than 300 characters and 800 sets by 5 animators [7][8]. Frame rate and joint counts unknown.

**Contributes.** A silhouette rule set that survives three technologies, and a measurable staging constraint (limbs off the outline) a verifier can check.

**Written.** [6] *Les Contes de la nuit* press kit (Ocelot and Mac Guff interviews); [7] *Dragons et Princesses*; [8] *Ciné si*.

**Video.** *(search)* [Princes et Princesses trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=an1f5nCi_fM); *(search)* [Tales of the Night trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4u1Ny3tkl4g).

**Copyright.** In copyright; no public-domain print.

## S03 · Noburō Ōfuji (`OF`) — chiyogami and coloured cellophane silhouettes

**What.** Japanese animator (1900–1961): *Bagudajō no tōzoku* (1926), *Kujira* (1927; colour remake 1952), *Yūreisen* (1956) [9][10].

**Methods.** Figures cut from chiyogami, woodblock-printed decorative paper, so the printed pattern is the surface (`surface.material_texture`) [9]. Silhouettes in the Reiniger and utsushi-e tradition (`surface.silhouette`). Coloured cellophane cut-outs on a backlit multiplane table: overlapping transparent sheets mix colours (`surface.translucent_colour`, `stage.translucent_layers`, `stage.multiplane`) [9]; chosen because cellophane, unlike glass or celluloid, is cheap and can be cut and pasted [9]. No dialogue (`speech.pose_only`): music and effects; the 1927 *Kujira* was synchronised to a gramophone record (`sound.score_led`, `timing.music_count`) [10].

**Cues.** *Baguda*: 14 min, silent, 18 fps [11]; *Kujira* 1952 about 8 min [10]; plane count unknown.

**Contributes.** Subtractive colour overlap of translucent parts, a surface operation (multiply blend) no other source in the set relies on.

**Written.** [9] National Film Archive of Japan, "Inventing Colored Cellophane Silhouettes"; [10] Nishikata review of *Whale* (1952); [11] NFAJ catalogue page for *Baguda*.

**Video.** [NFAJ streaming page, *Baguda*](https://animation.filmarchives.jp/en/works/view/15479); [Wikimedia Commons, *Kujira* (1952)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kujira_(1952).webm); [Commons, *Burglars of Baghdad Castle* (1926)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Burglars_of_Baghdad_Castle_(1926).webm).

**Copyright.** Commons marks both as public domain in Japan (Japanese films before 1953); US status not checked [E]. Strong candidates for a public-domain study clip of translucent colour, subject to the legal check.

## S04 · Wan Guchan and Shanghai paper-cut film (`WG`) — jianzhi pian

**What.** Shanghai Animation Film Studio's paper-cut films: *Zhu Bajie Eats Watermelon* (1958), *Fishing Child* (1959), *Ginseng Baby* (1961); later Hu Jinqing's *Snipe-Clam Grapple* (1983) [12][13].

**Methods.** Materials tested for figures and joints (plywood, aluminium, card, copper, paper); joints made with pellets of medical rubber tape between two paper layers instead of wire (`rig.hierarchy`) [12]. Motion planned before shooting: range, speed, force and trajectory pre-marked on the platform after a first test failed [12]. Three layers: carved foreground on glass, jointed puppets in the middle, sprayed scenery behind (`stage.multiplane`, `surface.perforation`) [12]. Figures move laterally on one plane; depth comes from scale and coordinated layer and camera moves (`stage.profile_strip`, `camera.multiplane_truck`) [13]. Visual sources: shadow puppetry (piyingxi) and folk paper-cut [12]. Later "picking": torn paper edges that blur contours into the background, leading to ink-wash paper-cut (`surface.soft_edge`) [12].

**Cues.** *Zhu Bajie* about 20 min, prepared from early 1957 [12]; *Ginseng Baby* 26 min; frame rate and pieces per puppet unknown.

**Contributes.** Pre-planned motion paths (a timing document before shooting), and the torn edge as a soft-edge method for cut-out.

**Written.** [12] Animation Obsessive, "Magic Paper" (citing Pu Yong); [13] Li, "Depth as Political Form in Mao-era Cutout Animation" (MCLC, 2026).

**Video.** [*Zhu Bajie Eats Watermelon*, subtitled](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=316XRGq_yQE) (linked from [13]); [Chinese Film Classics page](https://chinesefilmclassics.org/pigsy-eats-a-watermelon-1958/); *(search)* [*Ginseng Baby*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jk0PathxIk0).

**Copyright.** Studio-owned; China's film term may have lapsed there, US status uncertain [E]. Not a study-clip candidate without a legal check.

## S05 · Wayang kulit (`WK`), with Karagöz — shadow puppetry

**What.** Javanese and Balinese shadow-puppet theatre, a living tradition (UNESCO Representative List 2008) [14][15]; Turkish Karagöz as the contrast [16].

**Methods.** About five pieces of rawhide: one piece for head, torso and legs, each arm two pieces, joints at shoulder and elbow held with bone pins (`rig.hierarchy`) [14]. A central horn rod carries the body; two thin rods tied to the hands move the arms, so the puppeteer places the hand and the forearm and upper arm follow: inverse kinematics by hand (`rig.rod_driven`) [14]. Perforations chiselled into the hide read as lace in shadow (`surface.perforation`); both sides painted with fixed colour codes per character type, though the shadow shows only outline and holes (`surface.silhouette`) [14]. The carved face never changes; expression is the angle and movement of the figure (`face.posture_only`). Profile face on frontal shoulders (`swap.fixed_profile`). A cloth screen, a lamp, and a banana-trunk rack where waiting figures stand (`stage.profile_strip`). The figure is carried across the screen (`loco.glide`). Karagöz figures are translucent oiled hide that throws coloured shadows, jointed at neck, arms, waist and knees and moved by one rod at the neck (`surface.translucent_colour`) [16].

**Cues.** Puppets 25–75 cm [15]; arm rods about 37 cm, the body rod tied at 14 points [14]; Karagöz figures 35–40 cm [16]. Performances run dusk to dawn [15].

**Contributes.** The canonical rod-driven rig: a rigid body with two-segment arms posed by their end points. It is the clearest argument for an IK-like constraint in a cut-out rig.

**Written.** [14] Stewart, conservation study of wayang kulit puppets (ANAGPIC 2009); [15] *Wayang kulit*; [16] *Karagöz and Hacivat*.

**Video.** [UNESCO, Wayang puppet theatre](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pfydro4X2t0) (embedded on [the UNESCO page](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/wayang-puppet-theatre-00063)).

**Copyright.** The tradition is public domain; recordings are not. Study the construction, not a recording.

## S06 · Yuri Norstein (`NO`) — multiplane painted cut-out

**What.** Soviet/Russian animator (Soyuzmultfilm): *Hedgehog in the Fog* (1975), *Tale of Tales* (1979). Measured statistics: `cutout_styles_research.md` §4.6 [1].

**Methods.** Figures painted on celluloid, limbs cut apart, blank cel left round the figure (`rig.flat_pieces`, `surface.material_texture`) [17]. Glass planes about a metre deep, one every 25–30 cm, each movable sideways and towards the camera (`stage.multiplane`, `camera.multiplane_truck`) [17]. Fog from layers of tracing paper, not a blur: depth by translucency (`stage.translucent_layers`) [18]. Soft edges rather than hard cut lines (`rig.mask`, `surface.soft_edge`). Little or no lip sync (`speech.pose_only`, `face.posture_only`). Mixed ones and twos [1]; slow dissolves (`transition.dissolve`); sparse sound (`sound.score_led`).

**Cues.** Measured 59% ones, 29% twos, 9% threes, 45% identical frames, 21% of steps with camera drift [1]; ten minutes of *The Overcoat* in two years by three people [17].

**Contributes.** Translucency and soft edges as the depth cue, and the multiplane push where planes scale by their distance.

**Written.** [17] *Yuri Norstein*; [18] Animation Obsessive, "A Guide to Yuri Norstein"; [19] Kitson, *Yuri Norstein and Tale of Tales: An Animator's Journey* (2005).

**Video.** [*Hedgehog in the Fog*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mQqgViGenvk&t=60) (the measured window).

**Copyright.** In copyright; no public-domain print.

## S07 · René Laloux and Roland Topor, *La Planète sauvage* (`FP`) — cut-out in phases

**What.** French–Czechoslovak feature (1973), animated at the Jiří Trnka Studio in Prague from Topor's designs, character graphics by Josef Kábrt [20][21].

**Methods.** "Papier découpé en phases": characters drawn on thin bristol board, inked, cut out, each cut drawing one phase of a movement, so a gesture is a replacement series of whole drawings rather than a jointed puppet (`swap.pose`) [20]. Some parts still moved as pieces, which gives a stiff, deliberate motion (`rig.hierarchy` partly) [21]. Pen-and-ink cross-hatching close to etching (`surface.engraving`); large bare spaces dotted with figures (`stage.flat_graphic`). Score-led (Alain Goraguer).

**Cues.** About four to five years in Prague [20][21]; phases per second unknown. The Canopé notebook gives a shot breakdown with DVD timecodes (the Draag expedition at 37:21) [20].

**Contributes.** The bridge between articulated cut-out and drawn animation: whole-pose replacement while keeping a printed, engraved surface.

**Written.** [20] Canopé, *Cahier de notes sur… La Planète sauvage*; [21] Moins, "René Laloux: the man who made La Planète sauvage" (AWN).

**Video.** *(search)* [Trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MrAB7BaLl4E); *(search)* [Criterion Channel](https://www.criterionchannel.com/fantastic-planet).

**Copyright.** In copyright (Argos); archive.org uploads are not public domain.

## S08 · Smallfilms: Oliver Postgate and Peter Firmin (`SF`) — cut-out for TV at speed

**What.** *Ivor the Engine* (1959; colour 1975–77) and *Noggin the Nog* (1959–65), made in a cow shed in Kent [22][23].

**Methods.** Watercolour card cut-outs jointed with pins (later Blu-Tack), laid flat on painted backgrounds and moved by hand one frame at a time under a 16 mm Bolex (`rig.flat_pieces`, `surface.material_texture`) [22][23]. A motorised capture trigger made the rate possible. Figures carried across the frame rather than walked (`loco.glide`); pans over painted landscapes (`camera.move_over_still`); profile staging (`stage.profile_strip`). Postgate narrates and voices; no lip sync (`speech.narrator`, `speech.pose_only`); a bassoon is Ivor's whistle (`sound.sfx_carry`).

**Cues.** Moves in 1/25 s steps at 25 fps [22]; about 2 min of finished film per day, "twelve times a conventional studio" [22]; episodes of 5 and 10 min [22].

**Contributes.** A one-animator pipeline whose economy comes from narration instead of lip sync and gliding instead of walking: the cheapest complete cut-out method set.

**Written.** [22] *Smallfilms*; [23] Animation Studies blog, "Recollecting Ivor the Engine (1959)"; [24] Pallant, *Beyond Bagpuss* (book).

**Video.** [The six black-and-white *Ivor* films](https://archive.org/details/ivor-black-and-white); [Postgate on *Noggin*, BBC Archive](https://archive.org/details/twitter-774897794871988224).

**Copyright.** In copyright; the archive.org uploads are not public domain.

## S09 · Captain Pugwash (`PW`) — live lever cut-out

**What.** John Ryan's BBC series from 1957; the first episodes were performed and broadcast live [25][26].

**Methods.** Card characters on a painted background moved by hidden levers, pulleys and paper fasteners **while the camera rolled**: no frame-by-frame, continuous motion (`timing.live_performance`, `loco.glide`, `rig.flat_pieces`) [25]. Mouths opened and closed by a lever (`speech.mouth_flap`). One actor (Peter Hawkins) voices everyone, hidden from the operators (`face.performance`). Locked camera on each "caption" (`camera.locked`).

**Cues.** Four operators per caption; up to 50 scenes and about three weeks per 5-min episode [25][27].

**Contributes.** Real-time puppetry of cut-outs: the timing is a performance, not a timeline. The ancestor of S27 (live Character Animator).

**Written.** [25] *Captain Pugwash*; [26] "Animated Pirates"; [27] Skwigly, "A Rye Look at Captain Pugwash".

**Video.** [Isabel Ryan demonstrates the levers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8hsXok8csNE).

**Copyright.** In copyright.
