# Study the masters, family B: collage and photomontage

*2026-10-01 · part of [`../study_the_masters.md`](../study_the_masters.md) (an#223) · sources S10–S14 · entry format and tags as in [family A](a_cutout_film_and_silhouette.md)*

Collage is the family whose **art** is found rather than drawn. Its methods are mostly about replacement and juxtaposition, not rigging. For `an` the legal question comes first: public-domain engravings and photographs (pre-1931 publications in the US, life + 70 years elsewhere) are usable, and faithful scans add no new copyright (US: *Bridgeman v. Corel*, 1999; EU: DSM Directive Art. 14) [1]. Sources: Old Book Illustrations [2], the British Library's public-domain Flickr images, Wikimedia Commons (check per file).

## S10 · Terry Gilliam (`GI`) — Monty Python cut-outs

**What.** Animations for *Monty Python's Flying Circus* (1969–74). Measured statistics: `cutout_styles_research.md` §4.4 [3].

**Methods.** Found photographs and engravings recombined; limbs cut from photographs and pinned (`rig.photo_parts`, `rig.flat_pieces`, `surface.engraving`) [3][4]. "Swift, sudden movements are much simpler": snaps, drops, pop-ups and slides from the frame edge (`timing.pop_and_hold`, `loco.glide`) [4]. Objects transform into other objects, often across a cut (`swap.metamorphosis`, `transition.match_morph`). Locked camera, lit from above (`camera.locked`). Hinged or swapped mouths on photographic faces (`swap.part`). Comic SFX (`sound.sfx_carry`); period-type title cards (`text.card`).

**Cues.** Measured: 77% one-frame and 17% two-frame change intervals, 35% identical frames, 2 cuts/min (a floor), mean shot 20 s, median global shift 0.03 px [3].

**Contributes.** Photographic parts plus metamorphosis as the main verb: the shot is the gag, and the gag is a transformation.

**Written.** [4] Open Culture, "Terry Gilliam Reveals the Secrets of Monty Python Animations"; [5] Gilliam, *Animations of Mortality* (1978); [6] Christie (ed.), *Gilliam on Gilliam* (1999).

**Video.** [Cartoon Religions Ltd.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jDReTBFTxC8&t=0) (the measured window); [BBC *Do-It-Yourself Film Animation Show*, "Table-Top and Cut-Outs" (1974)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IrsKPKjGF_Y), Gilliam demonstrating the technique.

**Copyright.** In copyright. Gilliam's method, applied to public-domain engravings, is what `an` can legally reproduce.

## S11 · Harry Smith (`HS`) — *Heaven and Earth Magic*

**What.** *Heaven and Earth Magic* (No. 12), made alone in New York about 1957–62; black and white, now 66 min [7][8].

**Methods.** Cut-outs from 19th-century catalogues and engravings on a black ground (`rig.single_piece`, `rig.photo_parts`, `surface.engraving`) [7]. Dream-logic substitution of one object for another instead of a story (`swap.metamorphosis`). A locked animation stand (`camera.locked`). A musique concrète track of water, clocks and birds, with each figure's sounds deliberately displaced onto other images (`sound.sfx_carry`) [8]. Intended for performance with colour filters and shaped masking slides in front of the projector, so the frame's outline changes (`fx.frame_mask`) [9].

**Cues.** 66 min; the original said to have been about 6 hours [7]; four projectors intended (film, mask slides, filter slides) [9].

**Contributes.** A fixed engraving vocabulary on black, and the frame mask as a separate compositing layer over the whole picture.

**Written.** [7] *Heaven and Earth Magic*; [8] Senses of Cinema, "Alchemical Transformations"; [9] Nyback on projecting the film.

**Video.** [Heaven and Earth Magic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LWYzQn8FKpo); [with a live score by Deerhoof](https://vimeo.com/77367952).

**Copyright.** In copyright (Harry Smith Archives).

## S12 · Stan VanDerBeek (`VB`) — collage with live action

**What.** *Science Friction* (1959) and *Breathdeath* (1963–64), New York [10][11].

**Methods.** Found images from books, magazines, newspapers and newsreel stills cut and moved frame to frame (`rig.photo_parts`) [11]. *Breathdeath* cuts up newsreel film itself and mixes cut-out with staged live action, such as a figure whose head is a television (`stage.live_action`, `swap.metamorphosis`) [10]. *Science Friction* adds direct animation, found footage, Fischinger-like abstraction and live footage of the artist [12]. The structure follows the dance-of-death woodcuts, as a fast, rough montage. Head and mouth replacement on photographs is likely but not confirmed by a written source (`speech.photo_mouth`) [E].

**Cues.** *Breathdeath* 14:33 [11]; *Science Friction* about 9 min, 16 mm colour [12].

**Contributes.** Cut-out and live footage in the same frame, as satire; Gilliam's direct precursor.

**Written.** [10] Film-Makers' Coop catalogue (with the artist's note); [11] Light Cone; [13] Buchan, "A Curious Chapter in the Manual of Animation" (*Animation*, 2010).

**Video.** [*Breathdeath*](https://vimeo.com/215285724); [*Science Friction*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZRcppZ5Sg7I).

**Copyright.** In copyright (the estate); distributed by the Coop, Light Cone and LUX.

## S13 · Walerian Borowczyk and Jan Lenica (`BL`) — Polish graphic cut-out

**What.** *Dom* (1958, both), Lenica's *Labirynt* (about 1962) and *A* (1965), Borowczyk's *Les Jeux des anges* (1964) [14][15][16].

**Methods.** 19th-century photographs, engravings and illustrations as cut-outs; in *Dom*, Marey chronophotographs broken into parts and moved jerkily (`rig.photo_parts`, `surface.engraving`) [15]. Movement and montage "as simplified as possible", deliberately non-naturalistic (`rig.single_piece`) [14]. Selective colour on monochrome engraving in *Labirynt*; chimeras assembled from animal and human parts (`swap.metamorphosis`). One graphic sign per film: in *A*, one man and one letter (`text.kinetic`, `stage.flat_graphic`) [14]. *Les Jeux des anges* is camera moving over gouache paintings room by room (`camera.move_over_still`, `transition.continuous`) [16]. Mixed media in *Dom*: live action, pixilation (`stage.live_action`). Electronic and concrète scores.

**Cues.** *Labirynt* 14 min; *A* 9:57; *Jeux des anges* 13 min [14][16]. Frame data unknown.

**Contributes.** Primitive mechanical motion as an aesthetic, and the letter or sign as the protagonist.

**Written.** [14] Harvard Film Archive, "Freedom Outside Reason: the Animated Cinema of Jan Lenica"; [15] "Dreams Built By Hand" on *Dom*; [16] Bass, on *Les Jeux des anges* (EEFB).

**Video.** [*Dom*](https://archive.org/details/dom-the-house); [*A*](https://archive.org/details/a-1965); *(search)* [*Les Jeux des anges*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mq7ugNkM7Cg).

**Copyright.** In copyright; the archive.org items are user uploads.

## S14 · Frank and Caroline Mouris, *Frank Film* (`FM`) — density as the method

**What.** *Frank Film* (1973), Academy Award 1974, National Film Registry 1996 [17][18].

**Methods.** Magazine images cut out, glued onto acetate cels and shot on a stand (`rig.single_piece`, `rig.photo_parts`) [17]. Images in geometric patterns cascading up, down and across the frame, appearing and disappearing constantly: replacement at very high density (`swap.metamorphosis`) [18]. Images linked by visual rhyme, pie to desserts, televisions to appliances (`transition.match_morph`). Two narration tracks at once: a linear autobiography and a free association of words beginning with "F" (`sound.dual_voice`) [18].

**Cues.** 11,592 images; about five years of collecting; 8:50 [17][18].

**Contributes.** Many replacements per second organised as flows, and two voices in counterpoint.

**Written.** [17] *Frank Film*; [18] Yale Film Archive programme notes; [19] Moving Image Archive News, "Keeping Frank and Caroline Mouris Animated".

**Video.** [*Frank Film*](https://archive.org/details/frankfilm_201705).

**Copyright.** In copyright (Mouris / Pyramid Films); the archive.org copy is a user upload.
