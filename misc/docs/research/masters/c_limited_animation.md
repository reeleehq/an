# Study the masters, family C: limited animation and replacement shortcuts

*2026-10-01 · part of [`../study_the_masters.md`](../study_the_masters.md) (an#223) · sources S15–S20 · entry format and tags as in [family A](a_cutout_film_and_silhouette.md)*

These are drawn, not cut out, but their economies are cut-out economies: a held body with a few animated levels **is** a flat rig with swap channels, and their reuse banks are what an action library is. They supply most of the replacement, timing and reuse methods.

## S15 · UPA (`UP`) — design in motion

**What.** United Productions of America, Burbank: *Gerald McBoing-Boing* (1950), *Rooty Toot Toot* (1951), *The Tell-Tale Heart* (1953), Mr. Magoo [1][2][3].

**Methods.** Strong key poses, "pops and holds" with few in-betweens, called "simplified" rather than limited (`timing.pop_and_hold`, `swap.pose`) [1]. Only the body part that matters is animated (`timing.sectioning`). Backgrounds reduced to a few lines with no floor or horizon; flat colour fields that change with the mood (`stage.flat_graphic`, `stage.world_coding`) [1]. *Gerald*: sound effects replace dialogue (`sound.sfx_carry`) [3]. *Rooty Toot Toot*: the story told in song (`timing.music_count`). *The Tell-Tale Heart*: almost no character animation, a narrated film of camera moves over still paintings, dissolves and sudden zooms (`camera.move_over_still`, `transition.dissolve`, `speech.narrator`, `timing.freeze_frame`) [2].

**Cues.** *The Tell-Tale Heart* 7:24 [2]; hold lengths unknown.

**Contributes.** Colour as a mood channel separate from the set, and a whole film made of camera moves over paintings plus a voice.

**Written.** [1] Animation Obsessive, "The Animator Who Inspired Cartoon Network"; [2] Animation Obsessive, "How UPA Injected Horror into Cartoons"; [3] *Gerald McBoing-Boing*.

**Video.** *(search)* [*Gerald McBoing-Boing*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dveLSXkEpbI); *(search)* [*Rooty Toot Toot*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ejLWX1Dwn00); [*The Tell-Tale Heart*](https://archive.org/details/the-tell-tale-heart-1953).

**Copyright.** Not known to be public domain; the archive.org item's CC BY label is the uploader's own and is not a clearance.

## S16 · Hanna-Barbera TV (`HB`) — planned animation

**What.** *Ruff and Reddy* (1957), *Huckleberry Hound* (1958), *The Flintstones* (1960) [4][5].

**Methods.** A character split into a handful of levels: a held body cel, a head level, a mouth level, sometimes an arm (`rig.flat_pieces`, `swap.split_face`, `timing.sectioning`) [4]. Collars, neckties and bow ties hide the seam between head and body cels: a costume convention that makes a flat rig's joint invisible [4]. Dialogue on a held cel while the mouth level changes shape (`swap.part`, `speech.mouth_chart`, `timing.per_part_cadence`) [4]. Walk cycles over panning backgrounds that repeat (the same light socket six times) (`loco.walk_in_place`, `loco.legged_cycle`) [4]. Cycles and backgrounds reused across cartoons (`swap.bank`). Dialogue recorded first, then a pose reel (`sound.voice_first`) [4].

**Cues.** 1–2 drawings per foot against MGM's 16 per foot [4]; about 2,000 drawings per 7-minute short instead of 14,000 [6]; a Boo Boo walk of 8 drawings held 2 frames each (`timing.stepped`), over a background loop of 32 frames [4]. The "Hanna-Barbera mouth chart" circulates as 6 basic + 3 optional shapes (the Rhubarb A–H, X chart `an` uses) or 8 shapes; neither count rests on a primary Hanna-Barbera document [7][8].

**Contributes.** The layered TV rig itself, with the costume seam, and the walk in place over a repeating pan.

**Written.** [4] Yowp, "The Hanna-Barbera Tricks"; [5] *The Ruff and Reddy Show*; [6] *Hanna-Barbera*.

**Video.** [*The Flintstones* on Tubi](https://tubitv.com/series/300006856/the-flintstones) (a licensed stream); the frame grabs in [4] show the levels.

**Copyright.** In copyright (Warner Bros.).

## S17 · Zagreb school (`ZG`) — reduced animation

**What.** Zagreb Film, about 1956–1980: Dušan Vukotić's *Ersatz* (1961, Academy Award), Vatroslav Mimica's *Alone* (1958) [9][10][11].

**Methods.** Characters hold still while attention moves round the frame, then slide between poses with uncanny smoothness, sometimes on ones with even spacing and no slow-in or slow-out (`timing.pop_and_hold`, `timing.even_glide`, `loco.glide`) [9]. Geometric, anti-realist design, Klee and Suprematist influence (`stage.flat_graphic`). Usually no dialogue; music used "as music is in ballet", never mickey-moused (`sound.score_led`, `timing.music_count`, `speech.pose_only`) [9]. In *Ersatz* the whole world is inflatable, and transformation replaces locomotion (`swap.metamorphosis`) [11].

**Cues.** Shorts went from 12,000–15,000 drawings to 4,000–5,000 [9]; *Ersatz* 9:36 [11].

**Contributes.** Even-spaced glides between long holds: a timing signature that `step_hz` cannot produce and the style lint can measure.

**Written.** [9] Animation Obsessive, "Don't Call It 'Limited' Animation" (citing Holloway, *Z is for Zagreb*); [10] canmom, Animation Night 136, "The Zagreb School"; [11] Zagreb Film, *Surogat* (*Ersatz*).

**Video.** [Zagreb Film's official restorations channel](https://www.youtube.com/@zagrebfilmanimation/videos); *(search)* [Mimica, *Alone*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CdRdC1Oqmg8).

**Copyright.** Zagreb Film holds the rights.

## S18 · Mushi Production, *Tetsuwan Atom* / Astro Boy (`MP`), with Osamu Dezaki

**What.** Osamu Tezuka's Mushi Pro, Tokyo; *Tetsuwan Atom* on Fuji TV from 1 January 1963, 193 episodes [12][13].

**Methods.** "Three-frame shooting": drawings held three frames, characters mostly on twos and modulated to threes or slower (`timing.stepped`, `timing.per_part_cadence`) [12]. Pull-cels: a still cel slid across a background (`loco.glide`, `loco.walk_in_place`); pans over still images (`camera.move_over_still`) [12]. Sectioning: only part of the body moves (`timing.sectioning`) [12]. The **bank system**: stock poses, flying and walk cycles, reactions and whole cuts reused across episodes; episode 138 reused footage from nine earlier ones (`swap.bank`) [12]. Mouth flaps over a static face (`speech.mouth_flap`). Smears painted directly on cels (`fx.smear`). Electronic sound effects carry motion that is not drawn (`sound.sfx_carry`). Dezaki's "postcard memory": the frame freezes and dissolves into a painted rendering of the same image at a dramatic beat (`timing.freeze_frame`) [14].

**Cues.** About 1,500 drawings per episode, roughly a tenth of full animation [13]; on threes is about 8 drawings per second at 24 fps [12]; three mouth positions [E, weak source].

**Contributes.** The reuse bank as the centre of production, and the painted freeze frame as a dramatic beat.

**Written.** [12] Animétudes, "The History of Mushi Pro"; [13] review of Steinberg, *Anime's Media Mix* (*IJoC*); [14] "What Exactly Is a Postcard Memory?".

**Video.** [Official Tezuka Productions upload, *Astro Boy* (1963)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cXYXMQbN1uk); [episode list, Tezuka Osamu Official](https://tezukaosamu.net/en/anime/subtitle/a0030/index.html).

**Copyright.** Tezuka Productions.

## S19 · Clutch Cargo and Syncro-Vox (`CC`) — a live mouth on a still drawing

**What.** Cambria Productions, Hollywood, from 1959; also *Space Angel* (1962) [15][16][17].

**Methods.** Syncro-Vox (US patent 2,739,505): the actor's head held still, lips made up, filmed through an oval matte and superimposed on artwork whose mouth area is blank (`speech.photo_mouth`, `stage.live_action`) [16]. Comic-strip still cels on which only the mouth moves (`timing.sectioning`); motion faked with camera trucks, dollies and pans and with motorised glass panels (`rig.single_piece`, `camera.move_over_still`, `loco.glide`) [15]. Real smoke and flame superimposed optically; working models and live footage of aircraft (`fx.real_material`) [15]. Cels kept for reuse (`swap.bank`).

**Cues.** About 1,000 cels per half hour against about 1,000 per minute for full animation; as little as 12% single-frame photography [15].

**Contributes.** A photographic mouth on a still face, and real-material effects instead of drawn ones: the far end of "animate only the mouth".

**Written.** [15] Tralfaz, "How They Made Clutch Cargo"; [16] the Syncro-Vox patent; [17] *Clutch Cargo*.

**Video.** [*Operation Moon Beam*](https://archive.org/details/ClutchCargoOperationMoonBeam); [*Space Angel* episode collection](https://archive.org/details/Space-Angel-Episode-Collection).

**Copyright.** Both archive.org items carry a Public Domain Mark applied by the uploader; the status is contested. A candidate study clip only after registration and renewal are checked.

## S20 · Squigglevision (`SQ`) — line boil

**What.** Tom Snyder Productions: *Dr. Katz, Professional Therapist* (1995–2002), *Home Movies* season 1 (1999) [18][19].

**Methods.** Each outline traced loosely several times; five near-identical drawings looped so held poses never stop moving (`surface.line_boil`, `timing.ambient`) [18]. "Economy of motion": a helicopter scene costs what a living-room scene costs. Audio first: improvised sessions edited into scenes, stutters kept (`sound.voice_first`) [19]. A stockpile of generic drawings reused (`swap.bank`). Held poses with mouth changes (`swap.part`, `timing.sectioning`).

**Cues.** 5 drawings per boil loop [18]; loop rate and mouth count unknown.

**Contributes.** Line boil as a whole-frame "alive" layer independent of the acting.

**Written.** [18] *Squigglevision*; [19] It's Nice That, on *Dr. Katz* (Annette Cate).

**Video.** *(search)* [*Dr. Katz* playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL5bS5ggw_TZTKP5APxS8-tg0Emzs_I0tM).

**Copyright.** Comedy Central / Paramount.
