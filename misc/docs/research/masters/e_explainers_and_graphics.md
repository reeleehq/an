# Study the masters, family E: explainers, infographics and graphic motion

*2026-10-01 · part of [`../study_the_masters.md`](../study_the_masters.md) (an#223) · sources S28–S32 · entry format and tags as in [family A](a_cutout_film_and_silhouette.md)*

Here the narrator and the information carry the film, and characters are optional. These sources supply most of the text, diagram, draw-on, camera-over-canvas and narration methods, and they are the ones a data-viz or math-viz genre would share with cut-out (design principle 3).

## S28 · OverSimplified (`OS`) — narrated history, burst and hold

**What.** YouTube history series, made in After Effects and Photoshop. Measured statistics: `cutout_styles_research.md` §4.2 [1].

**Methods.** Blocky bodies, stick limbs, oversized heads with expressive brows and mouths, costume as identity (`rig.flat_pieces`, `face.expression_axes`) [2]. One narrator performs all the voices in the third person (`speech.narrator`) [2]. Burst and hold: moves on ones for a few frames, then long holds (`timing.pop_and_hold`) [1]. A "stage play" side-on staging (`stage.profile_strip`) [2]; crowds of identical figures (`stage.crowd`); push-ins on period prints and pans over maps (`camera.move_over_still`); date cards and territory maps (`text.card`, `text.map_diagram`) [1]. Hard cuts and bait-and-switch gags.

**Cues.** Measured: 91% one-frame change intervals with 59% identical frames, holds up to 63 frames, 9 cuts/min, mean shot 6.0 s [1].

**Contributes.** Burst-and-hold as a measurable cadence, and the narrated-map structure.

**Written.** [2] Creator Handbook, "OverSimplified: a YouTube empire".

**Video.** [The American Revolution, Part 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gzALIXcY4pg&t=300) (the measured window).

**Copyright.** In copyright.

## S29 · Kurzgesagt (`KG`) — never still

**What.** Kurzgesagt – In a Nutshell, Munich, from 2013; Illustrator, After Effects, recently Cinema 4D. Measured statistics: `cutout_styles_research.md` §4.3 [1].

**Methods.** Flat vector art broken into hundreds of layers, characters rigged with joints (`rig.flat_pieces`, `rig.hierarchy`) [3]. Continuous motion: twinkle, particles, glow pulses and parallax drift under the action (`timing.on_ones`, `timing.ambient`, `fx.particles`, `surface.glow`) [1]. A continuous virtual camera with pushes and parallax (`stage.multiplane`, `camera.multiplane_truck`); match-moves and flashes instead of cuts (`transition.match_morph`, `fx.flash`). Drawn-on lines, diagrams and labels (`fx.draw_on`, `text.map_diagram`). Narrator-led (`speech.narrator`).

**Cues.** Measured: 99% one-frame intervals, 24% identical frames, 7 cuts/min, 12% of steps with camera shift [1]; about 200 assets per 10-minute video [3].

**Contributes.** The opposite cadence to South Park: the hold is the exception. It also uses more methods than any other source in the set (20 of the 82).

**Written.** [3] 10.studio, "The Incredible Amount of Work Behind Kurzgesagt's Beautiful Animated Videos"; [4] Kurzgesagt, "About our YouTube channel".

**Video.** [The Paradox of an Infinite Universe](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=isdLel273rQ&t=60) (the measured window).

**Copyright.** In copyright.

## S30 · RSA Animate (`RS`) — drawing as the reveal

**What.** Andrew Park / Cognitive Media for the Royal Society of Arts, London, from 2010 [5][6].

**Methods.** Audio first: an RSA talk cut to about ten minutes, listened to many times, then sketched in numbered sequences (`sound.voice_first`, `speech.narrator`) [5]. Filmed live over the artist's shoulder drawing on a whiteboard wall; the hand stays in shot as the reveal device (`fx.draw_on`, `stage.live_action`) [5]. Each drawing sped up as plain video to land on its phrase (`timing.live_performance`) [5]. Black and red markers only, because other colours did not show on the early camera: a technical limit that became the style [5]. One large canvas, the camera following the hand and reframing across the argument (`camera.follow`, `camera.move_over_still`, `transition.continuous`) [E]. Drawings transform into the next idea (`swap.metamorphosis`); diagrams and handwritten words (`text.map_diagram`, `text.kinetic`).

**Cues.** About ten-minute films; *Changing Education Paradigms* 11:40 [6]; the speed-up factor is not published.

**Contributes.** Reveal by drawing with a visible hand, and one continuous canvas instead of shots.

**Written.** [5] Sketchnote Army, "Andrew Park transforms complex ideas into compelling visuals" (2025); [6] *Andrew Park (animator)*.

**Video.** *(search)* [Changing Education Paradigms](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zDZFcDGpL4U); [Internet Archive copy](https://archive.org/details/rsa-RSA_ANIMATE_-_Changing_Education_Paradigms) (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, so study only, no derivatives).

**Copyright.** CC BY-NC-ND: not usable for carving.

## S31 · Saul Bass (`SB`) — cut-paper titles and kinetic typography

**What.** Title sequences: *The Man with the Golden Arm* (1955), *Vertigo* (1958, spirals by John Whitney), *Anatomy of a Murder* (1959), *Psycho* (1960, animation by William Hurtz) [7][8][9].

**Methods.** *Golden Arm*: white paper bars on black under a rostrum camera, every appearance cut to Elmer Bernstein's beat, merging into the jagged-arm logo (`rig.single_piece`, `timing.music_count`, `swap.metamorphosis`) [8]. *Anatomy of a Murder*: a paper silhouette cut into seven body pieces that assemble and come apart, one credit card per piece (`text.card`), to Ellington (`surface.silhouette`, `text.kinetic`) [7]. *Psycho*: bars slid under the camera on two fields, only on, off, left, right, up and down, never crossing; split type slides together and apart (`timing.pop_and_hold`, `text.kinetic`) [7]. *Vertigo*: Lissajous spirals drawn by a converted anti-aircraft gun director, over live close-ups (`fx.draw_on`) [9]. A locked camera on a flat ground (`camera.locked`, `stage.flat_graphic`); the score drives everything (`sound.score_led`).

**Cues.** *Anatomy*: 7 pieces; *Psycho*: 2 fields [7]; *Golden Arm* under 3 min [8].

**Contributes.** Type and logo as the characters, axis-only motion, and cuts on the music's counts.

**Written.** [7] King, "Taking Credit", ch. 4 (Typotheque); [8] Art of the Title, *The Man with the Golden Arm*; [9] "Saul Bass and John Whitney" (Hitchcock's Vertigo).

**Video.** [*The Man with the Golden Arm* titles](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PhwsLS1XolU); [*Anatomy of a Murder* titles](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QccJ2L-7DVk); [*Psycho* titles](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aj6aBuC1Lb8). Each is the whole sequence.

**Copyright.** *The Man with the Golden Arm* is US public domain through non-renewal; an [Internet Archive print](https://archive.org/details/the-man-with-the-golden-arm_202407) exists, but Bernstein's score may be protected separately. A good public-domain candidate for a picture-only study clip of kinetic cut-paper, after the legal check. The others are in copyright.

## S32 · CGP Grey (`CG`) — the minimal explainer

**What.** YouTube explainer channel, from 2010 [10].

**Methods.** The voice-over is the spine; visuals cut to it (`speech.narrator`) [10]. Flat vector icons and a stick figure with glasses as the narrator's avatar (`rig.flat_pieces`); stills and stock footage mixed with motion graphics; maps and diagrams (`text.map_diagram`, `stage.flat_graphic`). Slide-like moves and pans across graphics (`camera.move_over_still`).

**Cues.** None published beyond running times.

**Contributes.** The floor of the explainer: what remains when animation is reduced to icons cut to a voice. A useful lower bound for an `an` explainer genre.

**Written.** [10] *CGP Grey*.

**Video.** *(search)* [Copyright: Forever Less One Day](https://youtu.be/tk862BbjWx4).

**Copyright.** In copyright.
